## Your job: referee the short expert companion

Read `repository/docs/expert_companion.md` and its PDF as a referee from the community whose papers it cites. Read the preprint first, so that you know what the companion is supposed to carry. Read the exposition and the long record only to check what the companion points to.

Judge in particular:

- **The one main point.** Does the first page say what was proved, why it settles the optimal exponent, and why the exponent is four, in terms an expert accepts at once? Is the idea of the construction visible before its details?
- **Attention.** Where does the paper spend pages on what these readers already know (Forrelation and its quantum algorithm, amplification, minimax, D = O(Q^4))? Where is it too quick on what they do not know: the per-column arrays and outward pointers, the tournament with off-promise rivals, the planted pair and the erased zero, the capped direct product with a joint event, and the order of quantifiers?
- **History and credit.** Check each attribution against the supplied papers at exact locators: cheat sheets (Aaronson–Ben-David–Kothari), the degree versus approximate degree paper and its cubic conjecture (Aaronson–Ben-David–Kothari–Rao–Tal), Forrelation and k-fold Forrelation (Aaronson–Ambainis), pointer functions (Göös–Pitassi–Watson; Ambainis et al.), Tal's 8/3 separation, Bansal–Sinha and Sherstov–Storozhenko–Wu, Huang's sensitivity theorem, and the 2026 comparison and certificate papers the preprint calls relevant. Say what each author of those papers would notice as missing, overstated or understated.
- **Length and order.** Would an expert finish it in one sitting? What should move to the long record?
- **The statement appendix.** Can an expert check, in a quarter of an hour, that the Lean statement encodes the theorem as stated? Are the displayed constants matched to the right kind of evidence?
