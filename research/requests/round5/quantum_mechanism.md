## Your job: the quantum upper bound in both papers

Read the quantum upper bound in the expert companion and in the exposition, and the corresponding sections of the preprint and the long record. Read as the authors of amplitude amplification, quantum search, search with bounded-error inputs, quantum minimum finding and king finding in tournaments would read it.

Judge in particular:

- **What is standard and what is new.** Is amplitude amplification with unknown success probability stated at the right level (a cited lemma with its exact source, or a proof the experts would skip)? Is the new part, a tournament in which comparisons are probabilistic, rivals may be off the promise and losers need not be ordered, given the care it needs?
- **The comparison.** Is it clear why one unordered-pair circuit gives complementary outcomes, why a genuine winner beats every rival at any address, and why cycles among non-winners do no harm?
- **The drift argument and exact sampling.** Is the drift identity stated and proved where a reader needs it? Is it clear that fresh workspaces give product weights, that amplification preserves the conditional law of the measured label, and how failure probabilities that depend on the history are coupled?
- **Retained state versus measured label.** Do both papers say exactly what the query bound uses and what is a separate, stronger statement about the state, with its formal status stated accurately?
- **Verification and the query count.** Is verification shown to be necessary (dominance without winning)? Is the polylogarithmic factor, including any displayed exponent, derived or attributed?
- **Placement in the literature.** Compare with Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp, Boyer–Brassard–Høyer–Tapp, Høyer–Mosca–de Wolf (and why it does not apply directly), Dürr–Høyer, Mande–Paraashar–Saurabh, and the 2026 papers of Ben-David–Kothari and Ambainis–Iraids–Kokainis at the locators where the preprint cites them. Would those authors recognise their results as stated?
- **Teaching versus refereeing.** Does the exposition's tournament example teach the mechanism to a student, and does the companion avoid re-teaching it to experts?
