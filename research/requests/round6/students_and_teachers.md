## Your panel: strong graduate students and the people who teach them

Read the exposition first, then the expert companion's opening and the long record's reading guide, as (i) a strong first-year graduate student in theoretical computer science who knows the query model and basic quantum computing from standard lecture notes, and (ii) a lecturer who teaches query complexity and would consider assigning the exposition. Anchor the background in the supplied survey and lecture notes and in the cited papers a student would be pointed to.

Press especially on:

- The first page and each section opening: does the student know why they are reading, where the argument is going, and what they will be able to do at the end? Where does the student first get lost, and why?
- Why each worked example is there, whether it is the right size, whether it carries through, and where an example is missing exactly where the student needs one (the tournament sampler, the planted pair, the direct product).
- What the exposition overweights (arithmetic, refinements, evidence bookkeeping) and underweights (intuition for why the exponent is four, why the obvious approaches fail, why the hypotheses are needed).
- Whether a lecturer could assign it: what prerequisites it assumes silently, which sections could be skipped, what exercises the examples suggest, and whether the links to the long record and to Lean help or distract a student.
- How the three documents work together as a reading path from first encounter to full proof, and where the transitions fail.
