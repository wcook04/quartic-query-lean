## Your panel: the randomized-lower-bound and separations community

Read all three documents, the expert companion first, as researchers whose cited papers built the classical side and the line of separations: cheat sheets, pointer functions (both the communication origin and the query-separation paper), the sensitivity theorem and its quantum implications, Tal's and Bansal–Sinha's and Sherstov–Storozhenko–Wu's Forrelation separations and the Fourier-growth work behind them, direct product theorems for randomized query complexity, and Yao's principle. Anchor each perspective in one of the supplied papers.

Press especially on:

- Whether the construction is presented as these authors would recognise and credit it: what is inherited from cheat sheets and pointer functions, what is new, and whether the documents say why the cheat-sheet route stopped at three.
- Why the documents spend what they spend on the planted pair, the erased-zero identity, the simulator, the capped direct product, the joint-event counterexamples, minimax and the Forrelation lower bound. Which would these readers consider standard, and which is the delicate new step that deserves the most care?
- Whether the direct-product statement and its separate caps would satisfy the authors of the direct product papers, and whether the comparison with their theorems is precise.
- Whether the Forrelation lower bound is credited and stated in the form this community uses, and whether the documents overweight a re-derivation they would simply cite.
- What these readers would ask about quantifier order, constants, the growing-k remark and the claim that the universal exponent is exactly four.
