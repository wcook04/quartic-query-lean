## Your panel: readers who are new to query complexity

Lead with the exposition and read every paragraph of it, then read the expert companion's introduction and the long record's reading guide and the parts of it the exposition links to. Read as three newcomers, one at a time, and keep their reactions separate:

1. a research mathematician from another field (say analysis or combinatorics) who has never studied query complexity or quantum computing;
2. a strong final-year undergraduate in mathematics or computer science who has met probability, linear algebra and algorithms but no complexity theory beyond P and NP;
3. a physicist or engineer who knows the circuit model of quantum computing from a first course but no theoretical computer science.

Anchor what each would know in the supplied lecture notes and survey, and look online for how the field introduces query complexity, Forrelation and separations to newcomers (lecture notes, textbooks, talks, blog posts, popular articles).

Press especially on:

- The first page and every section opening: does each reader know why they are reading, where the argument is going and what they will be able to do at the end? Exactly where does each reader first get lost?
- Every worked example: what it is for, whether it is the right size, whether it is carried through to the payoff, and every place where an example is missing (the tournament sampler, the planted pair, the direct product, why the exponent tends to four, why earlier separations stopped where they did).
- Every term and symbol: where it is first used, whether it is explained in ordinary language first, and whether the newcomer could say what it means afterwards.
- What the exposition overweights (arithmetic, refinements, formal-evidence bookkeeping) and underweights (intuition, motivation, pictures).
- After finishing: could each reader explain the main idea to a friend? Write down what they would say, and compare it with what they should say.
