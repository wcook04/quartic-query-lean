## Your panel: editors, referees and the reader who follows the funnel

Read all three documents, the long record in full or in very substantial part. Read as (i) a journal editor in theoretical computer science deciding whether the expert companion is ready for referees, (ii) a copy editor who marks every sentence they would change, (iii) a referee who wants to audit one claim end to end, and (iv) a lecturer deciding whether to assign the exposition. Use the supplied literature, the exemplar papers and the register rules as the standard, and search online for style guidance used by the field's journals and for widely praised expositions in theoretical computer science.

Press especially on:

- Sentence-level prose in all three documents: every sentence that reads as machine prose, padding, defensive hedging, house vocabulary, a list where an argument belongs, or that a copy editor would change. Quote them. Many dozens are expected.
- The funnel: whether the short paper and the exposition carry over the right material from the record, whether the three agree on every statement, constant, name and credit, and whether every link lands the reader where they need to be (follow at least ten claims from the exposition through the expert companion into the record and on to the Lean statement, and report each trip).
- The long record as a document: its reading guide, its navigation, its length, the reasoning it records, and whether a referee could audit it.
- The register measurement in `register/measurement.txt`: where it agrees with what you notice as an editor, what it misses, and what it flags wrongly.
- The writing method in `skills/` and `register/REGISTER_RULES.md`: which rules these documents still break, which rules are missing, and which rules seem to make the prose worse.
