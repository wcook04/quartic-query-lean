## 2. Amplification: the stopping bound and returned state both need proof

The amplification argument has two obligations:

1. Increase the chance of obtaining a successful result, with a fixed maximum number of preparation calls.
2. Preserve the normalized successful state of the original preparation.

The replacement proves both in ordinary mathematics.

Let

\[
\psi=A|0\rangle,\qquad
p=\|\Pi\psi\|^2,
\]

where \(\Pi\) projects onto success. When \(0<p<1\), write

\[
g=\frac{\Pi\psi}{\sqrt p},
\qquad
b=\frac{(I-\Pi)\psi}{\sqrt{1-p}},
\qquad
\sin^2\theta=p.
\]

The Grover iterate

\[
G=-AS_0A^{-1}S_\chi
\]

satisfies

\[
G^\ell\psi
=
\sin((2\ell+1)\theta)\,g
+
\cos((2\ell+1)\theta)\,b.
\]

Projection onto success therefore leaves \(g\) up to a phase whenever that branch has positive success probability. This is the relevant rotation mechanism in BHMT’s §2, Lemma 1 and equation (8). BHMT’s Theorem 12 is about amplitude estimation. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0005055v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For the packet’s capped construction, take

\[
M=\left\lceil\frac{10}{\sqrt\gamma}\right\rceil,
\qquad
N=\left\lceil
\frac{\ln(1/\eta)}{\ln(40/31)}
\right\rceil.
\]

In one trial, choose zero iterations with probability \(1/2\); otherwise choose an iteration count uniformly from \(0,\dots,M-1\). Run at most \(N\) fresh trials.

The exact averaging identity is

\[
\frac1M\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-1}
\sin^2((2\ell+1)\theta)
=
\frac12-
\frac{\sin(4M\theta)}{4M\sin(2\theta)}.
\]

BBHT gives this random-iteration identity in §4, Lemma 2. Its expected-time search theorem is a separate statement; the fixed cap used here still has to be established. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9605034v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

When \(\gamma\leq p\leq1/2\), the uniform-iteration part gives a trial success probability of at least \(9/40\). When \(p\geq1/2\), the zero-iteration half alone gives at least \(1/4\). Consequently,

\[
\Pr[\text{all \(N\) trials fail}]
\leq
(31/40)^N
\leq\eta.
\]

The implementation’s actual padded product satisfies

\[
N(2M+1)
\leq
138\,\gamma^{-1/2}\ln(1/\eta).
\]

That last point is a substantive accounting correction. A bound on a possibly smaller abstract procedure’s call count does not automatically bound the larger padded product charged by an implementation. The replacement bounds the product directly.

The endpoint cases are also explicit:

- At \(p=0\), failure is certain.
- At \(p=1\), success is certain.
- At every \(p>0\), successful conditioning is legitimate, since the first trial alone succeeds with probability at least \(p/2>0\).

### Why the output register matters

Consider the states

\[
|+\rangle|0\rangle
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\]

Measuring the first register in the computational basis gives the same distribution in both cases:

\[
\Pr[0]=\Pr[1]=\frac12.
\]

Their first-register density matrices are nevertheless different:

\[
\rho_{\mathrm{product}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/2\\
1/2&1/2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{Bell}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&0\\
0&1/2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

A measurement in the \(+/-\) basis returns \(+\) with probability \(1\) in the first case and \(1/2\) in the second.

This is an exact counterexample to the inference that equality of a classical label law determines the returned quantum state. I checked the calculation with rational density matrices. The included script also verifies that a CNOT uncomputes the copied label in this example and restores the product state, so the example does not suggest that state recovery is impossible.

### A useful refinement: controlled access to an opaque \(A\) is unnecessary

I made the coherent construction more explicit than the earlier draft.

Run all \(N\) fresh trial registers, record their success flags coherently, and then route the first successful trial’s entire original register into a fixed output slot. Each successful history has the same original-register factor \(g\); its phase and remaining records belong to auxiliary registers.

The variable iteration count can be implemented without assuming a controlled version of an opaque preparation unitary. A controlled Grover iterate can use **unconditional** calls to \(A\) and \(A^{-1}\), while controlling only the fixed reflections and the minus sign. On the false-control branch, the reflections are identities and \(AA^{-1}\) cancels. On the true-control branch, the operation is \(G\).

Thus the preparation-call schedule remains fixed, and the same padded cap applies. This supplies a concrete ordinary construction for the stronger formal target.

(I_H\otimes\Pi_J)\Psi_x=g_x\otimes\xi_x,
\]

with

\[
p_x>0
\Longrightarrow
s_x\geq p_x/2>0,
\qquad
\|\xi_x\|^2=s_x,
\]

together with the promised success bound, certain failure at \(p_x=0\), and compilation of that **same circuit** with the stated physical query cap. Its template must be fixed before the preparation and input are supplied. The return spells out these quantifiers and initialization requirements.

## 3. The probabilistic tournament works through weight drift

The comparison assumptions are

\[
0\leq a_{ij}\leq1,\qquad
a_{ij}+a_{ji}=1\quad(i\neq j),\qquad
a_{ii}=0.
\]

No transitivity assumption is needed.

Start with weights \(w_i=1\), and let \(W=\sum_iw_i\). An ideal step chooses pivot \(p\) with probability \(w_p/W\) and updates

\[
w_i\longleftarrow w_i a_{ip}.
\]

The central calculation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[W_{\mathrm{new}}\mid w]
&=\frac1W\sum_{p,i}w_pw_i a_{ip}\\
&=\frac{W^2-\sum_iw_i^2}{2W}\\
&\leq\frac W2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The equality pairs the terms for \((i,p)\) and \((p,i)\), using \(a_{ip}+a_{pi}=1\). It works for cyclic and fractional comparisons. At \(W=0\), the process is defined to remain at zero.

Now set

\[
T=\left\lceil\log_2(1000m)\right\rceil,
\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac1{1000T}.
\]

A dominant vertex \(s\) satisfies \(a_{sj}\geq1-\varepsilon\) for every \(j\neq s\). Until it is selected,

\[
w_s(t)
\geq(1-\varepsilon)^t
\geq1-t\varepsilon
\geq\frac{999}{1000}
>\frac12.
\]

Meanwhile, repeated drift gives \(\mathbb E W_T\leq m/2^T\). Therefore the ideal process misses \(s\) with probability at most

\[
\frac{2m}{2^T}.
\]

### The exact conditional law is the essential bridge

At a history with surviving weights \(w_i\), the quantum preparation has

\[
\Pr[\text{success and index }i]=\frac{w_i}{m},
\qquad
\Pr[\text{success}]=\frac Wm.
\]

Hence, when \(W>0\),

\[
\Pr[i\mid\text{success}]=\frac{w_i}{W}.
\]

Amplification preserves this conditional law exactly. A lower bound on the chance of producing *some* successful index would be insufficient: it would not justify using the ideal process’s weight calculation.

Before the dominant vertex appears, \(W>1/2\), so the stage success probability exceeds \(1/(2m)\). The capped amplifier consequently fails with probability at most \(\varepsilon\) on every relevant history.

Coupling the real process to the ideal one gives

\[
\Pr[\text{real list misses }s]
\leq
\frac{2m}{2^T}+T\varepsilon.
\]

The proof charges a disagreement only before \(s\) enters the list. Later failures cannot remove an existing entry. It uses conditional bounds at successive histories and a union bound; it does not require independent tournament-stage failures.

The list theorem has the correct order of quantifiers: **one sampler is chosen before the input and before the distinguished dominant vertex**. A membership indicator for a particular vertex is used only in the analysis.

### Exact cyclic example

The return includes this four-candidate instance, with

\[
m=4,\qquad T=12,\qquad \varepsilon=\frac1{12000}.
\]

Each entry is the probability that the row candidate defeats the column candidate.

| \(a_{ij}\) | \(s\) | \(a\) | \(b\) | \(c\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(s\) | \(0\) | \(1-\varepsilon\) | \(1-\varepsilon\) | \(1-\varepsilon\) |
| \(a\) | \(\varepsilon\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(0\) |
| \(b\) | \(\varepsilon\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1\) |
| \(c\) | \(\varepsilon\) | \(1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |

The three losing candidates form the certain cycle \(a\to b\to c\to a\).

After first pivot \(a\),

\[
(w_s,w_a,w_b,w_c)
=
\left(\frac{11999}{12000},0,0,1\right),
\qquad
W=\frac{23999}{12000}.
\]

The unamplified stage succeeds with probability

\[
\frac W4=\frac{23999}{48000}.
\]

Conditioned on success, the next pivot is \(s\) with probability \(11999/23999\), and \(c\) with probability \(12000/23999\). If it is \(c\), the only remaining positive weight is

\[
w_s=\left(\frac{11999}{12000}\right)^2,
\]

so the following successful pivot is \(s\).

The first-step expected total weight is exactly

\[
\frac{3\varepsilon+3(2-\varepsilon)}4=\frac32.
\]

The ideal process selects \(s\) within three pivots with probability one. The included rational-arithmetic check verifies normalization and drift across **23 reachable histories**. This is an illustration of the mechanism; the general result is established by the proof above.

## 4. Decoding and verification remain valid off promise

The delicate point in the comparison argument is how little must be known about an opponent.

A true winning column has an all-one \(X\)-block. Consequently, no pointer from an opponent can disqualify it, regardless of that opponent’s decoded address. When the winner’s **own** address is decoded correctly, its pointers disqualify every opponent. Since its own decoding succeeds with probability at least \(1-\varepsilon\),

\[
a_{i^\star j}\geq1-\varepsilon
\qquad(j\neq i^\star).
\]

That is the dominance property the tournament needs.

For verification, fix the candidate’s freshly decoded address. The local checks are exact Boolean tests on the raw input. Their number is

\[
K=m+(m-1)+r(S+2),
\]

and their maximum decision-tree depth is

\[
\max\{3,\ell+1\}.
\]

The depth qualification matters because a pointer check adaptively determines the coordinate it reads.

If \(h\) of these tests fail, uniform test preparation succeeds with probability \(h/K\), where “success” means finding a failed test. Therefore:

- If \(h=0\), search reports no failed test with certainty, and the verifier accepts.
- If \(h\geq1\), capped amplification with threshold \(1/K\) finds a failed test except with probability at most \(\varepsilon\).

Every nonwinner has a failed test at **every** possible address. Otherwise, passing circuit-consistency and output tests would establish a genuine winning address. Thus a nonwinner’s arbitrary decoded label causes no soundness gap.

For a true winner, correct decoding leads to acceptance with certainty. Its rejection probability is therefore at most \(\varepsilon\), coming from decoding. There is no additional search error on the correctly decoded winning address.

Combining these facts gives

\[
\Pr[\text{error on a zero input}]
\leq T\varepsilon
\]

and

\[
\Pr[\text{error on a one input}]
\leq
\frac{2m}{2^T}+T\varepsilon+\varepsilon
\leq\frac1{250}<\frac13.
\]

These guarantees survive conditioning on the preceding tournament transcript because verification is fresh and its guarantees hold for every fixed candidate and address.

The replacement distinguishes logical reads from ordinary physical bit-oracle queries. The compiler charges at most two physical queries per logical read.

For the tournament, if a comparison costs \(c_{\mathrm{rd}}\) logical reads, padding each stage to \(T\) comparisons gives

\[
B_{\mathrm{list}}
=
2T^2c_{\mathrm{rd}}N(2M_0+1)
\leq
6072\,c_{\mathrm{rd}}\sqrt m\,T^2\ln T,
\]

where

\[
M_0=\lceil10\sqrt{2m}\rceil.
\]

The constant follows from the padded amplification bound, then

\[
\ln(1000T)\leq11\ln T,
\qquad
\sqrt{2m}\leq2\sqrt m.
\]

The compiler factor of two is included once.

For the complete algorithm, let \(a=rnk\) be the address decoder’s logical-read count, and set

\[
c_c=2a+2(2t+1),
\qquad
c_0=\max\{3,2t+1\},
\qquad
M_1=\lceil10\sqrt K\rceil.
\]

The exact integer budget is

\[
B_{k,t}
=
T\bigl[2N(2M_0+1)T c_c\bigr]
+
T\bigl[2a+2N(2M_1+1)c_0\bigr].
\]

The first term pays for the padded tournament; the second pays for at most \(T\) fresh verifications. All branches obey this budget, including branches on which no amplification success promise holds.
