## 2. The hard steps that needed careful reconstruction

The complete proof is in `section.md`. These are the points on which its correctness principally depends.

### The rounded Gaussian chain really has a uniform zero endpoint

The proof uses
\[
\Psi(x)=\frac1{2\sqrt{2+x^2}},
\qquad
\varphi(x)=\frac{x(3+x^2)}{2(2+x^2)^{3/2}},
\qquad
\varphi'(x)=\frac3{(2+x^2)^{5/2}}.
\]
They satisfy
\[
|\varphi(x)|\le\frac12,
\qquad
0<\Psi(x)\varphi'(y)\le\frac3{16},
\]
and the transformed Gaussian integration identity
\[
\mathbb E[\varphi(Z)G(Z)]
=
\mathbb E[\Psi(Z)G'(Z)]
\]
for bounded continuously differentiable \(G\) with bounded derivative.

Take independent Gaussian layers \(U_j\), put \(V_j=HU_j\), and form the block biases
\[
w_s=
\bigl(
s\varphi(U_0),\
\varphi(V_0)\varphi(U_1),\
\ldots,\
\varphi(V_{k-3})\varphi(U_{k-2}),\
\varphi(V_{k-2})
\bigr).
\]
Conditional on the Gaussian layers, the signs are independent with these means.

At \(s=0\), the first block is unbiased. The remaining blocks can be cleared successively by reflecting a Gaussian layer across a Walsh row. Such a reflection flips the selected coordinate of \(HU\), preserves the other coordinates of \(HU\), and preserves the Gaussian law. It may change coordinates of \(U\), but those occur in the preceding block, which has already been cleared. Affinity of the multilinear extension then replaces the selected bias by zero without changing the expectation.

This establishes the whole uniform distribution at the zero endpoint. Merely showing zero individual means would not establish that fact.

### The three-block calculation exposes the cancellation

For \(k=3\), let
\[
w_s=
\bigl(s\varphi(U),\ \varphi(V)\varphi(U'),\ \varphi(V')\bigr),
\qquad V=HU,\quad V'=HU',
\]
and let \(F\) be the multilinear extension of the test function.

The first full coordinate derivative is
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial U_a}F_{0a}(w_s)
=
s\varphi'(U_a)\underbrace{F_{0a,0a}(w_s)}_{0}
+
\sum_bH_{ba}\varphi'(V_b)\varphi(U'_b)F_{0a,1b}(w_s).
\]
The repeated derivative vanishes by multilinearity. After the first Stein step, the earlier weight is \(\Psi(U_a)\varphi'(V_b)\), which is independent of the entire layer \(U'\).

The second derivative is
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial U'_b}F_{0a,1b}(w_s)
=
\varphi(V_b)\varphi'(U'_b)
\underbrace{F_{0a,1b,1b}(w_s)}_{0}
+
\sum_cH_{cb}\varphi'(V'_c)F_{0a,1b,2c}(w_s).
\]
Thus the final path weight is
\[
H_{ba}H_{cb}\,
\Psi(U_a)\varphi'(V_b)\,
\Psi(U'_b)\varphi'(V'_c),
\]
multiplying the derivative in the three selected coordinates.

For general \(k\), the invariant is that all accumulated weights depend only on earlier Gaussian layers. The next Stein operation therefore leaves them unchanged. That is what makes the induction work.

There is also a useful simplification: the interpolation average is a finite polynomial in \(s\), of degree at most \(m\). Its coefficients are bounded functions of the Gaussian variables. Differentiation under expectation can therefore be justified by averaging finitely many coefficients, without an additional limiting argument.

### Biased derivatives still contain higher uniform Fourier levels

An order-\(k\) derivative at a biased point does not isolate uniform Fourier degree \(k\). The exact identity is
\[
\partial_SF_g(\mu)
=
\sum_{T\supseteq S}\widehat g(T)
\prod_{i\in T\setminus S}\mu_i.
\]

The proof therefore needs a derivative estimate that is uniform over the relevant biases. It cannot simply discard the higher uniform degrees.

The replacement proves
\[
\sum_{S\in\mathcal J}|\partial_SF_g(\mu)|
\le
\bigl(16rd\ln(4n)\bigr)^{r/2}+2^{-r}
\]
for depth-\(d\) trees, \(|\mu_i|\le1/2\), and a family \(\mathcal J\) of distinct \(r\)-element coordinate sets.

The centered variables are
\[
\xi_i=\frac{z_i-\mu_i}{1-\mu_i^2},
\qquad
\mathbb E_\mu\xi_i=0,
\qquad
|\xi_i|\le2.
\]
The proof follows the adaptive query path and stops before a contracted coefficient becomes too large in absolute value. This preserves the centered-increment law. Conditioning on the future event that all coefficients remain small would invalidate that reasoning. The methodological credit belongs to the ordinary-tree discussion in Girish–Tal–Kewen Wu; the exact biased normalization and numerical constants are proved in the replacement. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.11604v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Combining this estimate with the path formula gives
\[
\left|\mathbb E_{\nu_1}g-\mathbb E_{\mathcal U}g\right|
\le
\left(\frac{3}{16\sqrt m}\right)^{k-1}
\left[
\bigl(16kd\ln(4km)\bigr)^{k/2}+2^{-k}
\right].
\]
There is no extra \(k!\): a path selects one coordinate in each labelled block, and different paths yield different coordinate sets.

### The moment and amplification arguments handle unpromised inputs

For the same hard and uniform distributions, the proof establishes
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal U}\Phi=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\mathcal U}\Phi^2=\frac1m,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\nu_1}\Phi\ge a_k:=18^{-(k-1)}.
\]

The hard-mean calculation uses marginal Gaussian second moments. It does not assume independence between a coordinate of \(U\) and its correlated coordinate of \(HU\).

Because neither distribution is wholly promise supported, the acceptance argument must be valid on the entire cube. If \(A\in[0,1]\) has promise error at most \(\varepsilon\), then
\[
(1-\varepsilon)(\Phi-\delta)
\le A
\le
\varepsilon+\frac{4\Phi^2}{\delta^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_1}A-\mathbb E_{\mathcal U}A
\ge
(1-\varepsilon)(a_k-\delta)
-\varepsilon-\frac4{m\delta^2}.
\]

Majority of \(2\tau\) independent runs gives error at most
\[
(8/9)^\tau
\]
and depth at most \(2\tau D\). With \(\tau=25k\), the dimension condition makes the required expectation gap at least
\[
\frac{119}{144}a_k.
\]
The supposed small-depth algorithm would instead have advantage at most
\[
\frac7{12}a_k.
\]
The difference is the strictly positive margin
\[
\frac{35}{144}a_k.
\]

That is the quantitative contradiction behind the \(2/18225\) theorem.

## 3. What the Bansal–Sinha issue actually shows

\alpha=1-\frac1k,\qquad L=\log(kN).
\]
The preceding level-\(r\) term has logarithmic exponent
\[
\frac{r-1}{2},
\]
whereas the next display uses
\[
\frac{\alpha r}{2}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
\frac{r-1}{2}-\frac{\alpha r}{2}

\frac{r/k-1}{2}.
\]
Thus the comparison loses the factor
\[
\boxed{L^{(r/k-1)/2}.}

At \(r=k\), the factor is one. At the largest level \(r=k(k-1)\), it is
\[
L^{(k-2)/2},

which grows without bound for every fixed \(k\ge3\).

Substitution makes every remaining logarithmic exponent nonpositive. For fixed \(k\), even this weaker logarithmic denominator still eventually beats the \((\log_2N)^2\) denominator required by the attached construction.

### The SSW comparison is stronger, with an explicit external premise

The relevant SSW theorem supplies an absolute \(C\ge1\) such that
\[
\sum_{|S|=r}|\widehat g(S)|
\le
C^r\sqrt{\binom dr}\,
(1+\ln n)^{(r-1)/2},
\qquad 1\le r\le n.
\]
The supplied v4 PDF labels the relevant results Corollaries 4.14–4.15; its HTML conversion labels them 4.12–4.13. The constant \(C\) is unspecified. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.10223v4?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The replacement explains the restriction transfer rather than merely quoting a resulting bound. Endpoint coordinates can remain free with probability \(1/2\), and interior coordinates with probability \(3/4\). Every path then has the same product of free probabilities. Combining its reciprocal with the sharp edge coefficient gives
\[
\left(\frac3{16}\right)^{k-1}
4\left(\frac43\right)^{k-2}
=
\frac3{4^{k-1}}.
\]

This yields the conditional comparison
\[
\boxed{
R_{(1-\eta)/2}(P_{k,m})
\ge
\frac{\eta^2}{162eC^2}
\left(\frac{m}{1+\ln(km)}\right)^{1-1/k}
}
\]
for \(k\ge2\), \(0<\eta\le1\), and power-of-two \(m\ge2^{15k+2}\).

The binomial factor is what supplies a factor of \(k\), after inversion, to cancel the order-\(k/\eta^2\) amplification cost. This is a proved ordinary comparison conditional on the external Fourier theorem. It is not the signature of either current local Lean endpoint.
