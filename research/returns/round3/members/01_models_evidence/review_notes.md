## 2. Concrete simplification attempts

The successful simplifications preserve the hypotheses or evidence distinctions that do mathematical work.

1. **Give the exponent argument immediately after the family bounds.** The draft formerly risked leaving the reader to infer why “nearly quartic” determines an optimal universal power. The return chooses $k$ for a given $\alpha$, displays the positive power gap and ratio, and explains the direction of the denominator inequality. This removes a deferred interpretive step while leaving the complete later parameter argument in §7. Moving declaration names and receipt detail to §2.7 made this calculation easier to read without dropping its evidence trail.

2. **Explain the construction by the winner contradiction.** A winning column points to a zero in every other column, so two winners contradict the all-ones condition. This short argument makes the total function and the role of pointers intelligible before the technical construction. The direct-product lower bound remains identified as the hard later step; the opening does not pretend that the uniqueness observation proves it.

3. **Use one finite witness and one finite obstruction for classical OR.** The three-tree mixture supplies the upper bound, while the uniform distribution on three inputs supplies the sharp obstruction and the optimal aggregate weights. This is shorter and more transparent than presenting a general linear-programming framework. The proof is specifically classical and cap one; no optimization claim about arbitrary quantum success is inferred.

4. **Keep unnormalized quantum vectors.** The measurement recurrence adds the two branch masses directly. Structural induction then proves nonnegativity and conservation without divisions or special handling of zero-probability branches. The tempting shorter description “normalize each branch and add” was rejected because it loses branch probabilities.

5. **Try, then reject, an idle-state explanation of deferred measurement.** The true invariant is acceptance mass, with a compatible embedding used in the query step. Replacing this by a claim that every completed state stays fixed would simplify the words but misdescribe the source proof. The returned explanation retains the two maps, the direct-sum measurement entry, and the finite-dimensional unitary extension; these are necessary proof interfaces rather than optional notation.

6. **Separate a minimum's existence from its representation.** A read-all tree gives a finite feasible budget; natural-number well-ordering supplies the minimum. The zero-query characterization then handles constants, empty promises and empty coordinate types uniformly. This avoids treating the source's infimum notation as an unexplained assumption of attainment.

7. **Avoid redundant statement proposals.** Existing advisory IDs were reused for the zero, identity, OR, parity, support, mass and cap checks. `ROOT-MASS` remains an inherited proposal whose source ingredients are distinguished from a freshly checked combined wrapper. `NCL-finite-capped` is an inherited more general finite-output-tree claim: the Boolean one-output case used here is a specialization, not an identical statement. Only genuinely additional ordinary claims receive new proposal IDs. `STATEMENT_CHANGES.json` remains `[]`, preserving the canonical statement registry.
