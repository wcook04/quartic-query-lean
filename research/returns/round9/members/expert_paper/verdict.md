## Suggested revision sequence

1. **Repair the dependency chain before polishing prose.** Resolve priorities 1–8 against the already available record passages. Confirm that the new wording connects the same assumptions, algorithm model and parameter schedule; do not add stronger results or new proof obligations.
2. **Reorder the account around the two reductions.** Settle the contribution statement and the quantum tool order, then expose the sampler transition and capped-predictor event. This can remove repeated warnings while making the actual invariant visible.
3. **Complete the quantitative and example interfaces.** Add the circuit convention, integer cutoffs, integer caps, short rounding chain and local sampling laws. Use exact examples only where they make an inference operational.
4. **Repair reader-facing evidence paths.** Check the few formal labels and historical comments that can mislead about scope. Review differing pinned editions for content, not just age. Where the destination already gives the precise caveat, do not add a redundant objection to the paper.
5. **Reflow and inspect after substantive edits.** Check the first-page contribution sentence, the two-clock paragraph and the diagrams at normal size. Avoid layout fixes that will be undone by paragraph changes. Use the low-priority findings selectively, guided by whether the repair reduces a real reader cost.

This sequence is an editorial plan, not authorization to rewrite the manuscripts. The return supplies advice only.

The review is a model simulation. The three specialist contexts ran in parallel, not serially one at a time. Generated references and image geometry were reviewed on the second pass. The classical log discloses limited early source exposure from a combined retrieval.
