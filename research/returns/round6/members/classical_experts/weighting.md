# Weighting for the classical separations panel

## What the numbers mean

The two short PDFs each have 14 pages. Their current spans were measured from the vertical position of each major heading to the next major heading, counting full page height. This allocates figures, intervening whitespace and the final page’s remaining area to a section; it is an approximate layout allocation, not an ink-area measurement. All 28 short-document pages were also inspected in rendered contact sheets. The proposed short-document budgets each total 14 pages. They are editorial judgments for this panel, not measured future layouts or a mandate to add prose until a quota is filled.

For the long record, SOURCE_STATUS.json declares that the PDF and current Markdown are not a reliable single-version pair. The review therefore uses the current Markdown for findings and estimates its reading load from 50,865 whitespace-delimited prose/math units, after removing comments, empty anchors, URL targets and reference-definition lines. Multiplying each share by the supplied PDF’s 125-page length gives **normalized page-equivalents**, not actual current section pages. Display-heavy mathematics and tables can change density substantially. No current record PDF page locator is inferred from that proxy.

For this audience, the long record deserves two routes: about 45 normalized page-equivalents of continuous argument, and the full preserved reference/proof record. The last table compares the complete record’s present allocation with the proposed **45-page-equivalent expert route**. Its percentages deliberately use different, labelled denominators. A zero route allowance means optional/reference access, not deletion. The complete independent proofs, claim catalogue and history remain available. These budgets implement the task’s audience-specific weighting question; they are not a general length rule for other papers.
