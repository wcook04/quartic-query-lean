# Advice for a reader interface over the supplied evidence

This is a design critique and inspection route, not an implemented interface. It uses the evidence already present. The central task is to let a reader compare **the actual ordinary claim, the actual formal statement and the stated relationship between them** without silently promoting one kind of evidence into another. [Reader principles 5, 7, 8, 10, 12 and 14; `repository/docs/statement_evidence_contract.md`]

## A bounded first visit

The method’s fifteen-minute target should be treated as a proposed budget for one question. It cannot stand for a complete review of standard-model adequacy, all ordinary proof steps or the alternative analytic Forrelation proof. A possible first question is: **“Does the public family statement choose one exact circuit before asserting both resource bounds, under the intended hard-cap models?”** [FX005, FX020, FX028; MF02]

| Proposed time | Reader action using existing material | What can be concluded at this stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minutes 0–3 | Open the family statement in `QuarticCorpus/Unconditional/Challenge.lean` and its configuration. Locate the circuit witness and the two resource inequalities. Compare the expert §1 statement and its explicit-constant note. | Identify the configured target, quantifier order, same-circuit requirement and existential logarithmic exponent. This does not prove that the definitions mean the standard objects. |
| Minutes 3–7 | Open `RLe`, `QLe`, `queryCap`, the oracle and the constant-on-promise branch. Read the corresponding record §§2.1–2.5 bridge destinations. | Identify a single algorithm before input, all-branch caps, the bit oracle, and which model-identification arguments remain ordinary. Complete review may require a longer visit. |
| Minutes 7–10 | Select one existing control: the true-only promise, OR2 threshold, or shared impossible-premise comparison. Read its independently expected answer and source definition. | See how a nearby easier model changes a concrete result. A passing finite case does not establish general model equivalence. |
| Minutes 10–13 | Inspect the solution forwarding and the four configured names; open the existing run record and review metadata as different objects. | Distinguish recorded execution, authored semantic relation, review freshness and reviewer identity. Do not infer one from another. |
| Minutes 13–15 | Write down the statement established under the chosen definitions and the remaining semantic question. Use record §5.5, §6.7 or §A.6 only if the chosen question depends on a route distinction. | Reach a bounded stopping point: the reader can state what was checked and what has not yet been inspected. This is a proposed task, not a measured performance claim. |

The same interface can offer other short tasks: inspect the realized-cap event; identify exactly which amplification interface the headline consumes; or compare the three universal-upper-bound routes. Each task should end at a named uncertainty or a well-specified conclusion. A complete chronological history is not a prerequisite for any of them. [FX011, FX031, FX045–FX046]

## Relationship types that must remain distinct

| Relationship | Evidence that can support it | What the display must not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Configured comparison | A named target in a particular configuration and its recorded accepted check, under specified definitions and allowed axioms | That the prose mapping is semantically right, that every nearby lemma was compared, or that two challenges have equal specification independence |
| Ordinary claim to formal type | A source-located authored review of the complete type, premises and definitions, bound to identified bytes where applicable | That a line link or a reviewer’s word “exact” is itself a kernel check |
| Statement-definition dependency | A definition used by a configured statement and the recorded relation vocabulary | That the definition independently receives theorem-comparison or semantic-validation credit |
| Formal dependency projection | The existing `Reach.lean` traversal and exported source/compiled-environment provenance | That displayed edges are necessarily direct proof-body references or that a public projection enumerates every dependency or axiom |
| Textual wrapper join | A source proof or backtick mention that names another declaration | That a type correspondence or comparison has been established for that join |
| Ordinary argument route | A curated link from a mathematical step to its proof and assumptions | That the checked formal proof uses this route merely because its endpoint agrees |
| Historical execution or review | A dated receipt or bound review with its original source identity | That a historical success is evidence for modified current bytes |

These distinctions already occur in `comparator_map.json`, `lean_links.json`, `Reach.lean` and the statement/evidence contract. The recommendation is to preserve their force when presenting them. It does not request new proof infrastructure. [FX009, FX012–FX013, FX027, FX033; MF03, MF06, MF09]

## Two supplied examples that test the interface design

### A wrapper join is not a checked type mapping

The supplied `repository/corpus/comparator_map.json` entry for `QuarticQuery.Checked.theorem_one_one` includes a statement association with `join = solution_proof_reference`, `comparison_credit = none`, and an explicit note that no type correspondence is recorded. A nearby relation field says `exact`, and the actual public theorem is separately compared. These facts must stay scoped to their respective relationships. A display that turns the wrapper join into an exact checked edge would misstate the evidence even if both endpoint texts were authentic. [FX013; comparator map, `declarations["QuarticQuery.Checked.theorem_one_one"].statement_ids`]

### A literal excerpt can still omit the decisive formula

The same map’s `QuarticQuery.QLe` primary gloss contains only the lead-in from record §2.4: “The predicate `QLe f q` is the following disjunction:”. The disjunction itself is needed to assess `ConstantOnPromise` and the program existential. An interface using that preview as a complete semantic explanation would omit the very clauses the reader needs. Show the complete existing display beside the exact Lean definition, and retain the source locator. This is a risk in consuming the supplied excerpt data; no implemented interface was supplied or observed to make this mistake. [FX006, FX036; comparator map, `declarations["QuarticQuery.QLe"].primary_gloss`; record §2.4]

Likewise, a missing gloss should remain missing. The sampled `Tournament.IsComparison` entry has no record gloss. The interface should not manufacture a source passage or assign a stronger relation to fill that gap. A reader can still open the formal definition and a clearly labelled curated destination. [Comparator map, `declarations["QuarticQuery.Tournament.IsComparison"]`; reader principle 8]

## Showing a mixed formal relation

The realized-cap endpoint is a useful test case. The companion already states a broader event conclusion for initially uncapped trees, a finite weighted-seed domain, and an ordinary identification between recursive compliance and query counts. These are separate axes. The record locally omits the last qualification. An informative comparison should keep the following together. [FX011, FX018, FX026, FX030, FX043]

| Axis | What the reader should see from existing sources |
| --- | --- |
| Reference claim | The precise separate-cap direct-product statement and the joint event it bounds |
| Formal domain | Finite weighted seeds, normalization and the tree/input types actually quantified |
| Conclusion | Success together with compliance, without assuming the original tree always stays capped |
| Resource representation | Recursive `actualComplies`, separately defined `actualPath`/`actualCount`, and the ordinary interpretation not proved equal in Lean |
| Ordinary transport | The existing finite-behavior reduction for arbitrary measurable external randomness |
| Relevance | Which version the headline lower bound uses and why its own distributions satisfy the needed restrictions |

The analogous amplifier comparison should separate retained-state conclusion, basis-label success predicate, ordinary general-projector reduction and measured-label headline use. The existing counterexample showing that equal label laws need not identify retained states is more informative than a single green badge. [FX008, FX035, FX041; record §§5.1, 5.5]

## Keep the edition attached to the claim

The evidence surface should distinguish the paper edition, formal-source pin, configuration/receipt identity, review source and binding state, and whether the displayed PDF represents the current Markdown. A human-review field should remain unperformed unless a person actually reviewed the object. The existence of a later automated textual review must neither disappear nor automatically refresh older bound records. [FX001–FX002, FX027, FX033; `SOURCE_STATUS.json`; evidence contract]

An old Appendix F sentence saying “now” is safe in its historical subsection and unsafe if detached from its date. A rendered PDF can be intact while representing an older text. These are provenance questions, not archive corruption or evidence that a theorem failed. [FX033; weighting measurement boundary]

## What belongs behind the first view

Keep full receipts, import details, source pins, historical chronology, all catalogue entries and dependency data available for inspection. The first view should answer the selected mathematical question and expose the decisive assumption. Stable catalogue IDs and hashed text should survive any derived view unchanged. A smaller print footprint can come from navigation and layout without deleting the recoverable record. [FX029, FX032; `weighting.md`, record Appendix B]

The interface should also say what its own checks establish. Source-hash matching and quotation validation establish file identity and locator fidelity. They do not prove semantic correspondence. Recorded theorem acceptance and an authored relation remain separately inspectable. This is the same distinction used by this return’s integrity manifest and source-reading statement.
