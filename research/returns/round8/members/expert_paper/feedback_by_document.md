# Review of the expert companion and its supporting documents

## Overall assessment and evidence boundary

The companion now explains enough of the construction to sustain a specialist’s attention through both principal reductions. Its best passages expose the precise facts on which the separation depends: comparisons need favor only the genuine winner; successful amplification must preserve a conditional label distribution; the lower-bound simulator predicts an address from a query’s position; and its success is bounded as a joint event. Those are substantial explanatory achievements. The next revision should improve the hierarchy of the account and the usability of its proof destinations while protecting these repairs.

The remaining objection is sharper than “too long.” The paper gives several different reasons to read it—reconstruction, explicit constants, a uniform parameter estimate, formal correspondence, and diagnostic examples—without consistently ranking them. A reader currently takes away that considerable work has been done to make the separation inspectable. The paper should make it easier to retain exactly two mechanisms, understand how they combine to give exponent four, and then choose which refinements to inspect. The headline separation, construction, and original analyses belong to OpenAI; explicit numerical choices and the stated uniform refinement need their own accurate descriptions. An explanatory contribution can be compelling without being presented as a new separation.

The three perspectives below are simulated specialist roles, not reports from named researchers. The coordinated review read the lead Markdown in full, inspected all sixteen PDF pages, followed the principal quantum and classical proof routes, compared selected primary literature, and checked the finite examples. No new mathematical blocker emerged within that scope. This is not a certification of the complete theorem or every analytic and formal dependency. Lean and Comparator were not rerun, and historical execution receipts were not re-audited. The reading log and source inventory distinguish full readings, targeted readings, sampled material, and inaccessible sources.

## Perspective 1: quantum-algorithms researcher

### What now earns confidence

The quantum argument has a recognizable operational spine. It constructs a comparison, uses a history of comparisons to sample candidates, and checks the resulting candidates against the total function. The winner-only guarantee is the right invariant, and the paper no longer asks an off-promise rival to behave as a reliable decoder. Sorting the pair before running one circuit makes the complementary comparison probabilities intelligible. These are mathematical contracts, not defensive qualifications that should disappear in a style pass. Preserve XP003, XP019, XP030 and XP033.

The amplification and tournament repairs are particularly valuable. The cap applies on every input and branch; direct measurement handles large success probability; zero success is allowed; the absorbing zero-weight state is explicit; and the coupling stops needing protection once the winner has been listed. The weighted example is explicitly abstract, without claiming an unrealized Boolean board. Fresh verification then separates selection history from the decision procedure. “no defect found” correctly describes a bounded-error search outcome. Earlier acceptance of a false candidate on a positive input need not be charged as a decision error. These details explain the proof rather than merely reassure the reader (XP027–XP028, XP038–XP039, XP042, XP044, XP047, XP049–XP050).

### Where the account still makes the specialist reconstruct an interface

Lemma 2’s label-law clause needs one local precision repair. The phrase referring to the original law under A leaves the conditioning implicit. The intended comparison is with A’s label distribution conditional on success; the accompanying proof supports that interpretation. State that conditioning at the interface, because the tournament uses a distributional guarantee, not just an amplified success probability (XP024). This is a wording issue with a specific mathematical consequence, not a discovered failure of the amplifier.

The local-test paragraph also carries too many jobs. Before the cost count, a reader needs to understand what a gate test reads: an actual address-block input bit when a predecessor is an input, and a stored transcript bit when a predecessor is a gate. Output consistency and the separate domain/value requirements then become clear. The current ingredients support this reading, but the expert should not have to infer the bit-oracle contract from the later implementation discussion. A similarly small clarification would identify which column contains each pointer-target bit in the two-way comparison. Neither repair calls for a larger circuit tutorial (XP020–XP021, XP029, XP051).

The overview introduces probabilities as weights before explaining that the algorithm never evaluates those numbers. In the proof, this distinction is handled well: fresh comparison workspaces produce a product, orthogonal labels make probabilities add, and amplification samples the resulting conditional law. Bring that operational distinction forward. A reader should immediately know that the matrix is an analysis of the circuit, not an additional oracle or a numerically computed data structure. Name the cost of rebuilding comparisons against the whole pivot history when explaining the square of the list length (XP008, XP032, XP039–XP040, XP045).

The order can also improve. The standard amplification lemma receives attention before the reader has encountered the concrete comparison that needs its exact law. A short application contract or a later placement would give that proof an immediate purpose. The comparison with minimum finding and tournament solutions should likewise be organized by the missing guarantee, not by successive names. In [Ambainis–Iraids–Kokainis, v3, §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11664v3), the explicit algorithm and surviving candidates make the operation visible before the analysis. That is the useful presentation comparison; it does not make its fixed-graph contract interchangeable with the present probability-weighted process (XP010, XP022).

Finally, three quantum links land too late or at an intermediate result: the decoder proof begins before `#forr_repetition`, the full tournament proof begins before `#upper-quantum-tournament`, and the exponential envelope for the explicit constant is established beyond `#upper-explicit-log6`. These semantic checks use the current packaged record; exact older pinned remote bytes were not compared. Correct destinations are part of the proof’s usability, not cosmetic hyperlink maintenance (XP046, XP052, XP058). Edition-sensitive BBHT locators also need the inspected arXiv version identified. The publisher/arXiv page-range disagreement should be resolved as a metadata discrepancy, without asserting that an uninspected journal lemma has the wrong number (XP059–XP060).

## Perspective 2: randomized lower-bound researcher

### The reduction is now visible

The strongest classical sentence explains that a query to the true cell names the hard answers by its position. This avoids the tempting but false pathwise claim that a successful execution must previously have learned the entire address. The abstract’s probability language, the independently selected planted column, the dummy-run analysis, and the unchanged address strings all support the intended distributional argument. Keep this chain intact (XP061, XP070–XP071, XP089–XP090).

Several other repairs deserve protection. A single promise-supported hard law is chosen before its independent copies. The direct-product proof shows why one query changes only one factor. The total cap of the simulator implies the required separate caps. A true-cell visit is controlled jointly with nonheaviness, rather than after conditioning on a favorable event. The union over columns is canceled by the independently uniform planted index. The lower bound’s three costs remain distinguishable. These are precisely the points at which a compressed account can accidentally lose the factor needed for the exponent (XP082, XP085–XP086, XP097–XP098, XP101–XP102).

### The existing generic result deserves a complete interface

The most consequential local weakness is the start of §4. The paper announces a reusable lower bound, but “with these choices” makes the reader recover its assumptions from a construction originally introduced with Forrelation-specific notation. The record already has the general statement. Expose its finite partial Boolean function, the circuit’s exact domain/value contract, the conventions for m and r, and the range 1 ≤ L ≤ m with L < R(P). Give that existing result a stable name or number and prove that statement. This requests no new theorem; it makes the theorem already being used extractable and citable (XP080–XP081).

Two related facts should become available earlier. Circuit size matters to the quantum cost but not to this generic lower-bound reduction. Independent hard instances also do not imply a uniform answer vector. Hardness controls shallow predictors, including constant-output predictors; a uniform-address or entropy argument is unnecessary. Clarifying these points would prevent a reader from remembering a simpler but different reduction (XP080, XP083).

The direct-product discussion is substantively good but arrives before the concrete predictor gives it a task. One sentence explaining what the predictor will receive, output, and be charged would orient the lemma. Preserve the sufficient global-budget route from [Drucker, v2, Theorem 1.1 and §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0644v2). The paper correctly acknowledges that its separate-cap proof is not the only sufficient route. Do not elevate that convenient proof organization into an indispensability claim (XP084, XP087–XP088).

### A few conditional steps deserve their own sentence

The erased-zero estimate invokes residual uniformity after an adaptive history. State or clearly route to the reason: before discovering a column’s zero, observed answers exclude queried rows without favoring one remaining row over another. The complete past is the conditioning object. This is more useful than another reminder that the chosen column is independent (XP091).

In the simulator, distinguish original query time from the counter of charged target queries. The free outside information includes variables that may look related to the target’s eventual cell, so identify its joint independence from the target address instances. The default output can accidentally be right; that is harmless because the argument needs event inclusion, not equality of success events. The existing record example explains this, but the link advertised as a worked example currently lands at the general simulator. Point it to `#CL-simulator-example` (XP094–XP096, XP099). These are small repairs with direct effects on whether a reader can reproduce the reduction.

The discussion of the earlier cheat-sheet recipe also needs a narrower claim. [Aaronson–Ben-David–Kothari, v2, §§3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01937v2) explains why certificate cost can destroy the useful quantum advantage. “any partial separation” should not suggest unconditional preservation of a useful gap. When deriving the cubic ceiling for this particular AND–OR recipe, explain why balancing its two quantum-cost terms at the chosen parameter is optimal for those estimates. That makes a limitation of the calculation intelligible without claiming a limitation of all cheat-sheet methods (XP064–XP067).

The uniform Forrelation refinement should remain available, but its position presently blocks the main conclusion. Its improved parameter dependence and weaker logarithmic dependence should both be visible; it does not uniformly dominate the cited supplier bound. Its analytic distribution also needs introduction before the reader confronts the acceptance-gap calculation. Preserve the distinction between that distribution and the later promise-supported minimax law (XP103–XP110).

## Perspective 3: handling editor

### Establish the reason for a second paper more positively

The subtitle, opening credit, and explicit statement that the theorem, construction, and analyses are OpenAI’s are successful. The question appears early, the exponent is defined with the required uniformity, and the explicit logarithmic power and coefficient are labeled as reconstruction choices. These repairs answer the basic ownership questions (XP123, XP125, XP128–XP130).

What remains underdeveloped is the reader’s benefit. The stated intention to explain how the two difficult reductions fit together gives a topic and a purpose. The article should identify the inspections it enables: recovering an implementable exact weighted sampler, checking the capped address predictor without conditioning away difficult inputs, and following their common construction into the exponent argument. Those are more persuasive reasons to read than an inventory of derivations and formal endpoints. The comparison with the supplied OpenAI introduction supports an explanatory contribution, not a claim that the two reductions originate here (XP124, XP131; XP063).

The theorem citation and the numerical choices also need clean visual ownership. A citation to OpenAI’s Theorem 1.1 beside a display with this reconstruction’s particular constants can momentarily attribute the whole strengthened-looking package to the source. The following clarification helps, but the statement should distinguish the source result from the selected explicit implementation at the point of use (XP129). The same discipline is needed in §5: attribute the uniform estimate as the reconstruction’s calculation using identified antecedents, explain its purpose, and avoid language suggesting a new separation or an established priority claim for the refinement (XP180–XP181; XP104–XP105).

### Allocate attention before polishing sentences

The introduction spends too much of its first encounter on a predecessor’s four-parameter accounting and then repeats the chronology of exponents. The calculation has a legitimate purpose: it explains what the new construction must escape. Its present position delays the object whose improvement the reader came to understand. Compress the chronology, state the certification obstacle before the predecessor costs, and place detailed historical accounting after the present construction has acquired meaning (XP132–XP134).

The comparison catalogue in §1.3 creates a similar problem. Back pointers, tree variants, direct-pointer alphabets, alternative Forrelation suppliers, minimum finding, kings, and imperfect checking all matter differently. Retain the distinctions that explain an actual design choice—especially why no zero carries a pointer and why separate bit fields avoid the alphabet objection. Route more specialized comparisons to the relevant reduction. [Ambainis et al., v3, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.04719v3) supplies a genuine predecessor obstacle; it should illuminate the present access model rather than become another item in an ancestry list (XP139–XP142).

The largest structural repair is to complete the fixed-k consequence before the optional uniform-in-k analysis. The paper itself says the cited fixed-k theorem suffices. Nevertheless §5 introduces a new analytic apparatus, and §6 imports its explicit threshold into the main closing argument. This makes an optional refinement look logically necessary. The reader should reach exponent four along the sufficient route already established, then elect to inspect the explicit threshold and growing-k family. Keep the uniform calculation, its source credit, and the warning that the result is not an exact Ω(Q⁴) bound (XP179, XP184–XP186; XP053–XP054).

### Keep the final sections useful to their intended reader

The formal section should answer which printed claim corresponds to which formal statement, under which model, and with which strength. Its distinction between existential family bounds and the separate explicit-circuit theorem earns its place. The measured-label interface also directly serves Lemma 2. The retained-state variant, finite-seed taxonomy, nonempty-minimum construction, and “ordinary bridges” currently compete for similar attention. Route the auxiliary detail through precise destinations, and replace the vague bridge description with the existing implication and its status. Do not request more formalization or imply that source availability, a successful build, a Comparator result, and human review are interchangeable (XP187–XP191; XP055; XP114–XP115).

Appendix A contains several excellent witnesses, especially sign loss, domination without certification, joint independence, and the distinction between joint and conditioned success. Its eight examples would be easier to use if grouped by the inference they protect. Preserve the examples’ calculations; reconsider how much space the more generic quantifier reminder needs beside a main proof that already gets that order right (XP193–XP195; XP056–XP057).

The PDF repairs must not be undone or reported again as unresolved: the title keeps “is four” together, the earlier Figure 1 crossing-label problem is fixed, and Appendix A is readable full-width prose. Remaining figure criticism concerns small explanatory text at normal reading size and the clarity of schematic conventions. Long repository URLs and commit text in the bibliography are persistent residuals already acknowledged in the previous round, not new discoveries.

## Prioritized integrated revision route

**First, agree on the paper’s explanatory contract and route.** Make the concrete benefit of the reconstruction explicit; distinguish source results, selected constants, and the uniform refinement. Reduce duplicated chronology and ensure the current construction appears before the historical calculation consumes the reader’s working memory. This is the controlling decision for later cuts (XP124, XP129, XP132–XP134, XP180).

**Second, protect the sufficient theorem path.** The construction, quantum bound, generic classical bound, cited fixed-k supplier, and exponent deduction should form one continuous route. Place the optional uniform estimate and growing-k schedule after that conclusion or clearly mark a detour that can be skipped. The schedule can be motivated by its existing balance of losses; its numerical arithmetic belongs at the detailed destination (XP179, XP184–XP186, XP053–XP054, XP103).

**Third, repair interfaces before adding explanations.** State the classical generic contract completely; specify the amplifier’s conditioning; make local gate reads explicit; identify analytical weights as quantities produced implicitly by the circuit; and make the simulator’s cap, time index, free information, and default output easy to separate. These fixes should replace ambiguity with a small amount of information, not trigger new background sections (XP081, XP020, XP024, XP039–XP045, XP094–XP096).

**Fourth, repair all four semantic proof destinations together.** A valid anchor is insufficient if it lands after the promised definition or before the promised numerical conclusion. Check the decoder, complete tournament proof, exponential constant envelope, and worked simulator example against what each linking sentence actually promises. Preserve a visible way back to the short argument. This is an immediate, bounded improvement with substantial benefit to a referee (XP046, XP052, XP058, XP099).

**Fifth, perform one selective register and rendering pass after reallocation.** Split paragraphs that perform several mathematical jobs, not every paragraph above an arbitrary length. Keep contrasts that rule out a specific false inference and numerical constants that support a stated interface. Group counterexamples, simplify formal-status prose, identify citation editions, and improve small figure labels. Do not treat zero flagged phrases or shorter sentences as evidence that the paper’s hierarchy is fixed. A final pass should verify that earlier repairs remain visible, particularly all-branch caps, exact conditional sampling, the stopped coupling, the one-sided promise, the shared circuit, and joint-event accounting.

## How the supporting documents serve the companion

### Exposition

The exposition generally supplies the slower operational explanations the expert paper should not duplicate. Its three-budget account, simulator example, and explanations of residual uniformity help recover the classical mechanism; its quantum walkthrough helps interpret weighted sampling. These are appropriate destinations for a reader who needs another encounter with the construction. They should not carry an unstated hypothesis essential to the specialist theorem.

Two service points remain. Keep the exposition’s explicit logarithmic power aligned with the companion’s distinction between the selected circuit and an existential family theorem. In the exercise on a true-cell visit and direct products, the opening answer should immediately identify the whole-path/nonheavy joint event; a qualified conclusion should not require reading past an initially overbroad “Yes.” The later explanation contains the needed restriction (XP196–XP197). This is targeted funnel feedback, not a second full review of the exposition.

### Reasoning record

The record performs its intended role when it contains the exact preparation, conditional transition law, simulator trace, constant envelope, and uniform-parameter arithmetic that the companion can safely omit. The checked destinations often resolve the local uncertainty. Their size makes precise landing points especially important: a reader should not search backward through a long record to discover the definition promised by the link.

Do not expand the record in response to every short-paper complaint. Most recommendations above concern ordering, interface extraction, and navigation. Where the companion uses an unnormalized direct-product potential and the record also discusses a normalized value, distinguish the roles when directing the reader to that proof (XP122). Formal evidence should remain connected to the exact printed claim and labeled by its actual status. Nothing in this review calls for a new proof obligation, stronger bound, or new formal development.
