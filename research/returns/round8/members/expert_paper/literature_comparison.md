# Literature comparison

## Scope and main judgment

This comparison combines the three synthetic referee perspectives. It compares particular explanatory decisions, not the reputations or presumed opinions of named authors. “Read” below always refers to the stated passages; it does not imply that every proof in a source was audited. The source inventory in `feedback.json` and the reading log provide the fuller division of work. No new theorem, sharper bound, formalisation task or priority claim is requested.

The companion's strongest passages already improve the reader's ability to inspect the two reductions. They expose the exact comparison law, construct the product-weight sampler, extract an address predictor from a proposed cell query, and preserve the full-path cap in the relevant joint event. Its main weakness against the better source passages is allocation. It explains several predecessors before fully explaining its own object, then inserts a substantial optional analytic refinement before completing the fixed-parameter consequence. The useful next revision is selective: make those existing arguments easier to find and use.

## 1. Give the companion its own contribution, with the theorem's ownership intact

The public listing is [the October 5 preprint directory](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/A-Nearly-Quartic-Separation-Between-Randomized-and-Quantum-Query-Complexity-October-5-2026); only its online metadata was inspected, while the actual reading used `paper/extracted.md`.

But ownership disclosure should lead to a positive explanation of this document's purpose: which steps become easier to inspect here, and how their statements relate to the formal development. The exact sampler and capped simulator provide concrete answers. A general promise of transparency would be weaker.

The source theorem also uses existential constants where the companion supplies explicit numerical choices. That difference should remain visible at the relevant statement. It is neither evidence of a new separation nor a reason to weaken the reconstruction's genuine value.

Its introductory deduction says “Combining these three inequalities yields”. The writing move is immediate payoff: finish a short consequence while its premises are live. The companion's fixed-k exponent deduction has that economy once reached. Moving its sufficient proof route ahead of the optional uniform analysis would recover the same continuity. The universal quartic upper bound remains an appropriate cited supplier; a new proof is unnecessary. [Primary edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12629v1).

## 2. Introduce a construction as necessary repairs, not an inventory

**Aaronson–Ben-David–Kothari, arXiv:1511.01937v2, introduction and §§3.1–3.2.** These passages were read closely; later proof material was not comprehensively reviewed. The crucial sentence in §3.1 is “But we cannot simply append the certificate to the input”. The preceding attempt has produced a problem, and the address array solves it. Before that, the source explains why naive totalisation creates a promise-testing obstacle and why the inner function supplies useful certificates. Each ingredient answers a question the reader already has. [Primary edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01937v2).

The companion's predecessor calculation is valuable: it makes the competition between decoding cost, verification cost and classical composition cost explicit. The placement is less successful. A specialist meets this calculation before having enough of the present construction to appreciate the alternative. Keep the quantitative explanation, but bring its motivating obstruction forward and its full arithmetic to the point of comparison.

This source also helps delimit two claims. A framework for totalisation does not automatically preserve a useful separation regardless of certificate-verification expense. Likewise, explaining the cubic outcome of the displayed AND–OR recipe is not a general impossibility theorem about all cheat sheets. Qualifying “any” and “stops” would align the prose with the calculations already supplied. No missing theorem is alleged.

The companion improves the informal story by treating guessed addresses explicitly. Reaching an addressed cell need not mean that an algorithm first computed the entire answer vector. The actual reduction extracts that vector from the query's coordinate. This more careful explanation should survive shortening.

**Ambainis–Balodis–Belovs–Lee–Santha–Smotrovs, arXiv:1506.04719v3, introduction and §1.3.** The panel read the technique discussion, including back pointers and balanced trees; the proof body was not audited. Under “Use of a balanced tree”, the source explains that “the tree allows ``random access'' to the highlighted zeroes”. It also identifies the alphabet-size cost of storing many direct pointers in one symbol. [Primary edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04719v3).

The companion now makes the corresponding engineering choice intelligible. Its zeros carry no outward navigation aid; pointers live in a hidden selected cell; a comparison reads one separately encoded field. Those are specific reasons a predecessor's randomized shortcut does not transfer and its direct-pointer objection is not decisive here. Preserve that precision. The first reading does not need a catalogue of every earlier pointer variant, and “highlighted zero” should not drift back into “any zero”.

## 3. State the quantum tool's contract before spending attention on its proof

**Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp, arXiv:quant-ph/0005055v1.** The quantum referee read the introduction and §2 through Theorems 2–4, including the geometry and expected-cost QSearch analysis; later estimation sections were not read. The useful pattern is a precise success event and an explicit statement of which cost is controlled. [Primary edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055v1).

The companion needs more than an expected-cost invocation: every branch has a cap, and successful amplification must preserve the preparation's conditional label law. Its explicit lemma is therefore justified. The lemma's unusual guarantee should lead, while routine geometry can remain compact. Say which conditional law is preserved, identify a constant mixture, and handle the initial success-probability-one endpoint. These are small contract repairs, not a request to restore numerical trial arithmetic removed in round seven.

BHMT makes the invariant successful direction visible in the rotation: amplification changes its coefficient, while preserving that normalized component. Its split by available knowledge then tells the reader which procedure applies. This supports a compact account of the companion's exact-law requirement, with the endpoint handled separately, instead of a generic appeal to amplification.

**Høyer–Mosca–de Wolf, arXiv:quant-ph/0304052, supplied extraction read in full.** Its bounded-error-input search assumes a candidate validity predicate with a controlled noisy implementation. That is an illuminating interface comparison. The present winner-only comparator does not assign each loser a reliable Boolean validity label, so that search theorem is not interchangeable with the tournament. The existing distinction is correct; it will be easier to assess after the reader has seen the comparator. [Primary identifier](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304052); the supplied extraction's version suffix was not independently established.

Their introduction says, “An obvious but sub-optimal quantum search algorithm is the following.” It then prices the natural error-reduction approach before offering the better procedure. The companion already makes the analogous explanatory move with nested candidate search and verification. The costs differ, but the shared benefit is to establish the obstacle before asking the reader to learn its remedy.

The companion's best quantum explanation is its operational passage from products to sampling. Tensoring fresh comparison workspaces multiplies acceptance probabilities for one label; orthogonal labels add their masses. The algorithm never calculates those weights. Keep these sentences next to the preparation. An opening that merely says the algorithm “uses” probabilities as weights temporarily suggests inaccessible numerical operations.

## 4. Let the product proof show what one query can change

**Drucker, arXiv:1005.0644v2.** The classical referee read the introductory theorem and method discussion, query definitions, §3 proof and fair-algorithm specialization; the editor read the introductory method. The online copy confirmed the edition and theorem. Later generalizations were not reviewed. [Primary edition](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0644v2).

The “fortune” language works because the source identifies an operation: one query can affect one instance's continuation value, while the untouched factors remain available under the product law. The companion's unnormalised recursion is a particularly good adaptation for specialists. It avoids division by the probability of a transcript, displays the common product factor, and isolates the one-instance inequality. Preserve the sentence attributing that factorization to the product law; it supplies the explanation, not redundant narration.

The current text also correctly observes that Drucker's global-budget result would suffice with a harmless loss here. This prevents a chosen separate-cap proof from acquiring an exaggerated necessity claim. Identify imported parameter names so the source's T and alpha do not silently collide with the companion's notation.

The classical reduction would be easier to reuse if its already-proved generic interface were stated in one place: finite partial problem and hard law, exact circuit role, inherited parameters, allowed cap, and conclusion. The requested edit exposes existing mathematics. It does not ask the authors to generalize their result.

The cited literature's intuitive hiding stories also make the companion's stronger accounting valuable. Independent hard inputs do not automatically give uniform answer strings; residual hidden-zero uniformity is conditional on the transcript; and eventual heaviness is a property of the terminal original run. The fixed-time predictor argument handles these distinctions. Its link should land on the existing positive simulator trace, complementing the appendix's examples of invalid shortcuts.

## 5. Separate the sufficient Forrelation supplier from an optional uniform refinement

**Bansal–Sinha, arXiv:2008.07003v3.** The panel read the introduction, exact promise, principal theorem and corollary, quantum interface, total-function consequence and opening technique overview. It did not audit the full lower-bound proof. The overview says “we first focus on the special case of $k=3$”. This makes the correlated distribution and its observable concrete before introducing the general analytic machinery. [Primary edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07003v3).

The companion's three-property interface improves usability for this total construction: the explicit Walsh instance supplies both a quantum decoder and a suitably small exact classical circuit. Its one-sided promise, sign preservation and distinction between an off-promise analytic law and a promise-supported minimax law are also worth keeping.

Section 5's signal/error/leakage display is useful, but it presents explicit constants before defining enough of the central correlated law to inspect their origin. Describing the truncation as different names a change without explaining its mathematical job. Supply a compact description or clearly identify the passage as an outline with a precise full-definition destination. Explain a numerical hypothesis through the term it controls.

The fixed-k cited result already proves the headline exponent. The uniform reconstruction serves additional explicit thresholds and the growing-k route. Label those roles distinctly, including the refinement's provenance. Its better polynomial dependence on k trades against logarithmic factors; it is not automatically a pointwise improvement in every regime. None of these observations establishes new priority.

**Tal, arXiv:1912.12561v1; Sherstov–Storozhenko–Wu, arXiv:2008.10223v4.** Root sampled Tal's abstract, introductory pages 1–4 and opening technique overview, and SSW's introduction/results plus “Limitations of previous analyses” and “Our approach”. Neither full proof was audited. [Tal edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12561v1); [SSW edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10223v4).

Tal's distinction between fixed constant k and a growing parameter, including the exponential amplification dependence, makes the quantifier order part of the exposition. The companion's fixed-k argument is already correct: choose k for the proposed exponent, then let the dimension grow. Preserve it, but complete that argument before asking the reader to assess a schedule that changes k. The growing route needs its own uniform costs and a brief reason for its selected balance.

SSW separates its partial and total consequences from an independently useful Fourier theorem. Its discussion explains the analytic change through a small special case before presenting the broader approach. This is a useful model for giving the companion's optional estimate an explicit identity and explaining what the changed ingredients buy. It is not a reason to replace the chosen Walsh supplier with another partial separation: the exact-circuit requirement is a separate construction contract. Both comparisons favor visible dependencies over a larger literature survey.

## 7. Examples, figures and the practical revision

The companion's fractional tournament example does work that a deterministic-survivor picture cannot: it makes the exact label law tangible. Its explicit boundary from a realized small Boolean input is essential. The construction and planting captions similarly say which structural operation the reader should see. These judgments concern their mathematical explanatory roles; the panel's rendered-page observations belong in the visual review.

The appendix is strongest when an example targets a tempting inference at an actual proof step: squaring loses the sign, dominance does not certify validity, and conditioning on a cap can destroy the relevant success bound. Generic cautions have lower priority for this audience. Select and group examples by the inference they protect, and give the principal positive simulator execution an equally usable route.

The editor also sampled the openings of the supplied cross-domain SK specialist paper and exposition (`exemplars/short-paper.md` and `exemplars/expository.md`), without auditing their mathematics. They name a particular mechanism before a dependency catalogue. That is a transferable ordering lesson. Their paragraph lengths, proof depth and notation are not templates for a query-complexity paper, and similarity to those stylistic features would not establish reader benefit.

The resulting advice is concrete: bring the current mechanism forward; state its existing interfaces completely; finish the fixed-k proof with its sufficient supplier; identify the optional analytic route and its provenance; preserve the exact sampling and joint-event arguments; and repair proof links to match their promises. These changes concern the effectiveness of the account, not the creation of additional mathematics.
