# Figure specifications

These are instructions for the team drawing the figures. No images are supplied. The seven figures follow the examples in `exposition.md`; their numerical data come from the deterministic JSON in `examples_output.txt`, generated by `examples.py`.

## Conventions for all figures

Preserve rational values as fractions. A JSON string such as `"3/8"` denotes an exact rational number. JSON paths below start at `$`; array indices in those paths are zero-based. Displayed column and row numbers are one-based. In a stored two-bit pointer or transcript, bit position 0 is the leftmost bit.

Use explicit labels, arrowheads, border styles, and patterns so each figure remains legible in black and white. Color may reinforce a distinction but must not carry it alone. Print the bit value inside every displayed bit box. Use monospaced digits for stored words, equal-width cells for comparisons, and horizontal fraction bars where space permits. Supply the caption and alt-text description with the final artwork.

Every finite construction here is an expository example. The drawings do not certify new Lean statements or demonstrate the asymptotic lower bound by enumeration. Figure-specific interpretation limits below should guide the artwork and its labels.

**Purpose.** Show why cyclic comparisons are compatible with the weight analysis and distinguish the ideal pivot process from a ranking or a bracket tournament.

**Layout.** Panel A is a directed graph of four candidates. Panel B is a table of the four first-pivot outcomes. Panel C is a compact table of the ideal miss probabilities. An optional, separately titled fractional panel can follow at its own passage.

\[
(a_{ij})=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1&1\\
0&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&1\\
0&1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Start Panel B at \(w=(1,1,1,1)\), \(W=4\). A pivot is recorded before its updated weight becomes zero. Each first pivot has probability \(1/4\).

| First pivot | Probability | New weights \((w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)\) | New total |
|---|---:|---|---:|

**Optional fractional panel.** Title it “Separate abstract comparator.” Candidate 1 always defeats every opponent; each pair among candidates 2, 3, and 4 is fair. This is a different matrix from Panel A.

|---|---|---|
| \(2\) | \((1,0,1/2,1/2)\) | \((1/2,0,1/4,1/4)\) |

## Figure 3. Amplification preserves the successful candidate law

**Placement.** Section 3, after `<!-- passage:amplification-example -->`, before `amplification-general`.

**Purpose.** Separate total success probability from the relative probabilities of successful outcomes.

**Layout.** Use two panels labeled “Before amplification” and “After one Grover iteration.” Each panel contains an amplitude row and a probability display over the ordered basis \(g_1,g_2,b\). Below both panels, repeat the same small conditional-distribution display for \(g_1,g_2\).

### Exact drawing data

| Quantity | Before, \(j=0\) | After one iteration, \(j=1\) |
|---|---|---|
| Amplitude of \(g_1\) | \(3/10\) | \(3/5\) |
| Amplitude of \(g_2\) | \(2/5\) | \(4/5\) |
| Amplitude of \(b\) | \(\sqrt{3}/2\) | 0 |
| Probability of \(g_1\) | \(9/100\) | \(9/25\) |
| Probability of \(g_2\) | \(4/25\) | \(16/25\) |
| Probability of \(b\) | \(3/4\) | 0 |
| Total success probability | \(1/4\) | 1 |
| Conditional probability of \(g_1\), given success | \(9/25\) | \(9/25\) |
| Conditional probability of \(g_2\), given success | \(16/25\) | \(16/25\) |

The probability of \(b\) is the square of its displayed amplitude, equivalently one minus the recorded success probability. Draw bars or areas using probabilities, and label amplitudes separately. Include the successful unit state

\[
|g\rangle=(3/5)|g_1\rangle+(4/5)|g_2\rangle.
\]

An arrow between panels may say “successful amplitudes multiply by 2; unsuccessful amplitude multiplies by 0.” Those multipliers apply to this \(p=1/4\) example. The exact reflection computation uses \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{3})\).

**Caption.** One Grover iteration raises success from \(1/4\) to one in this example. It scales the whole successful component, preserving the conditional probabilities \(9/25\) and \(16/25\) of its two outcomes. Amplification does not make the successful outcomes equally likely.

**Source attribution.** General invariant-subspace calculation: BHMT2002, §2, Lemma 1 and equation (8). Its role in the bounded sampler: OpenAI2026, Lemma 3.1. The displayed state and exact reflection calculation are the exposition's finite example.

**Computation JSON paths.** `$.amplification.basis`; `$.amplification.states[0]`; `$.amplification.states[1]`; `$.amplification.one_iteration_good_multiplier`; `$.amplification.one_iteration_bad_multiplier`; `$.amplification.checked_by`. Compute each displayed outcome probability by squaring the corresponding exact amplitude when it is not stored separately.

**Accessibility and interpretation.** Use solid, hatched, and unfilled bars for \(g_1,g_2,b\), with exact values printed alongside. Give both total-probability panels the same scale from zero to one. Label the conditional display “given success.” Do not equate amplitudes with probabilities, depict all marked outcomes as uniform, imply that one iteration succeeds for arbitrary \(p\), or suggest that the general algorithm knows its input-dependent success probability.

**Alt-text brief.** Before amplification, the two successful outcomes have probabilities nine hundredths and four twenty-fifths. After one iteration they have probabilities nine twenty-fifths and sixteen twenty-fifths, with no failure probability. Their relative probabilities given success are unchanged.

## Figure 4. Conditioning and enforcing a query cap

**Placement.** Section 4, beside `<!-- passage:and-example -->`, after the four-input execution table and before `capped-product`.

**Purpose.** Make the different probability denominators visible: conditioning on an early stopping event, bounding that joint event, and evaluating a simulator that always obeys the cap.

**Layout.** Use a pair of small decision trees above a shared four-row table. The left tree is the unrestricted algorithm. The right tree is the capped simulator. Under the table, place the clearly labeled probability statements.

### Exact drawing data

The input distribution is uniform on `00`, `01`, `10`, and `11`, and \(P(a,b)=a\land b\). The cap is \(L=1\). Each input has probability \(1/4\).

Left tree: query \(a\). On \(a=0\), return zero after one query. On \(a=1\), query \(b\) and return \(b\), using two queries. Right tree: query \(a\); on \(a=0\), return zero; on \(a=1\), stop before querying \(b\) and output zero. Keep the \(a=1\) branch visible in the right tree.

| Input | Original queries | Original output | Original correct? | Event \(E\): original uses at most one query | Capped output | Capped correct? |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| 00 | 1 | 0 | yes | yes | 0 | yes |
| 01 | 1 | 0 | yes | yes | 0 | yes |
| 10 | 2 | 0 | yes | no | 0 | yes |
| 11 | 2 | 1 | yes | no | 0 | no |

Visually bracket the first two rows as \(E\), without removing the other rows. Place the following labels below the table:

| Label | Exact value | Denominator to state |
|---|---:|---|
| \(\Pr(E)\) | \(1/2\) | All four equally likely inputs |
| \(\Pr(\text{original correct}\mid E)\) | 1 | Inputs retained by \(E\) |
| \(\Pr(E\text{ and original correct})\) | \(1/2\) | All four inputs |
| Best success under an enforced one-query cap | \(3/4\) | All four inputs |
| Capped simulator success | \(3/4\) | All four inputs |

If showing branch posterior values, use one after observing \(a=0\) and \(1/2\) after observing \(a=1\). An optional two-copy footnote may state that the joint early-stop success event has probability \(1/4\), while the optimal success under separate one-query caps is \(9/16\). Keep this footnote separate from the single-copy trees.

**Caption.** The original algorithm is correct on every input. Conditioning on its one-query executions retains exactly the easy branch, making conditional success one. A simulator that stops before exceeding the cap still faces the original input distribution and succeeds with probability \(3/4\). The lower-bound argument bounds the joint event under the original distribution.

**Source attribution.** Capped direct-product setting: OpenAI2026, Lemma 4.1 and §4.2; Drucker2012, §1.3 and Lemmas 3.1–3.2. The AND example and optimal finite values are computed for this exposition.

**Computation JSON paths.** `$.AND_conditioning.distribution`; `.L`; `.runs`; `.probability_within_cap`; `.probability_correct_given_within_cap`; `.probability_correct_and_within_cap`; `.single_copy_optimum`; `.capped_simulator_success`, all under `$.AND_conditioning`. Optional values: `.posterior_optimum_after_a_0`, `.posterior_optimum_after_a_1`, `.two_copy_all_nonheavy_joint_success`, and `.two_copy_optimal_adaptive_success_with_separate_caps`.

**Accessibility and interpretation.** Use a bracket and the label \(E\) for the retained rows. Mark the capped failure on `11` with the text “incorrect,” not color alone. The trees must show when a query is performed and when a result is output. Do not label conditional success one as the performance of a one-query algorithm on the original distribution, hide aborted branches, or call the cap event independent of the input.

**Alt-text brief.** An AND algorithm stops after the first zero but otherwise reads both bits. Its one-query executions are always correct. A second tree enforces one query on every input and fails on `11`, giving overall success three quarters.
