# What the long-record exemplar contributes

The useful comparison is a way of separating reader tasks. In its opening, the SK/QAOA exemplar identifies the mathematical conclusions, explains how the arguments connect, distinguishes the roles of the short paper and exposition, and states that the two selected formal statements do not assert QAOA circuit convergence. Its roadmap then sends readers through the mathematics in §§1–11, formal interfaces in §12, and execution/source observations in §13. The quartic record already has a valuable dependency map; preserve it and adopt this clearer separation when relocating repeated run history. This is a structural comparison, not evidence for any quartic claim. [Source: `exemplars/long-reasoning-record.md`, opening, lines 13–54; §2.1.]

Section 12 is useful because it explains what a reader must compare before inspecting a proof. It names the hypotheses and quantifier order, distinguishes exported conclusions from internal suppliers, and gives source destinations for the formal statements. It also identifies a specific ordinary deduction—the passage from sampled convex secants to an ordinary right derivative—without promoting that deduction to a separately named formal theorem. For the quartic record, the corresponding editorial task is to put the actual statement and its scope beside its source link, then identify which proof route and execution result support it. The exemplar supports this presentation principle; its mathematical translations cannot be imported into the quartic development. [Source: §12.1, lines 5150–5199; §12.2, lines 5207–5244; §12.3.]

Section 13 makes useful distinctions between exact finite calculation, agreement with source bytes, challenge compilation, implementation compilation, extraction, and comparison acceptance. Its dated observations are kept separate rather than merged into one successful-looking status. This supports a single, clearly designated evidence destination in the quartic record. It does not justify copying all of the exemplar’s chronology into the main proof: §13 is itself lengthy, and its placement and explicit boundaries are more transferable than its volume. [Source: §13, lines 5364–5443.]

The comparison has important limits. The exemplar mentions its companions in prose but does **not** supply actual reciprocal return links to the short paper or exposition in the inspected Markdown. It therefore cannot be cited as a demonstrated solution to the quartic return-navigation problem. Nor does its length establish an appropriate target length, or its reported evidence establish successful verification of this different theorem. The recommended return links follow from the present reader task. These observations come from reading the opening and §§12–13, sampling §2.1, and searching the full Markdown for companion references; they do not claim a complete mathematical or PDF usability review of the exemplar.
