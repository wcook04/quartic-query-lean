# Construction: source and attribution notes

## Closest antecedents and exact limits

The input-addressed certificate array is a standard cheat-sheet ingredient. Aaronson, Ben-David and Kothari define a certifying function and then the cheat-sheet totalization in **arXiv:1511.01937v2, §6.1, Definitions 14 and 15**. Theorem 16 and Lemma 17 address the corresponding query upper bounds. The packet's TeX-derived extraction misnumbers these definitions as 10 and 11, and its theorem and lemma as 12 and 13; the official HTML supplies the published v2 numbering. The local reading was `literature/quartic-ref-02-separations-in-query-complexity-using-chea/extracted.md:1640–1815`, checked against [the official v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.01937v2). This comparison supports attribution of the addressed certificate-array mechanism, and supplies no replacement proof for the present local-test signatures.

Direct pointers to a zero in every nonmarked column are also an explicit earlier idea. Ambainis, Balodis, Belovs, Lee, Santha and Smotrovs discuss them in **arXiv:1506.04719v3, §1.3, “Use of a balanced tree”**, immediately after the tree-traversal account. They explain that the direct-pointer variant makes the alphabet too large for their Booleanization objective. This is at `literature/quartic-ref-04-separations-in-query-complexity-based-on-p/extracted.md:274–275`, within the subsection at 260–275, and is visible in [the official v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1506.04719v3). The funnel adjudication's narrower observation about the preceding balanced-tree paragraphs remains correct, but citing only those paragraphs would omit this direct-pointer antecedent. No novelty claim should be made for “one pointer to each rival column.”

Ambainis, Iraids and Kokainis use closely related local certification in **Near-Optimal Separations of Certificate Complexity from Randomized and Quantum Query Complexity, arXiv:2609.11664v2**, the version held in the priority-5 annex. Section 3.1, Lemma 3.3 expresses a verifier as a conjunction of a fixed number of checks, each evaluated with logarithmically many deterministic queries. Appendix D.1 explicitly specifies the encoded fields, fixed check indices, checks of range before an encoded value is used as an index, and the adaptive reading bound. Section 3.2 applies the cheat-sheet construction and explains the all-zero certificate lower-bound argument. Local locators are `literature/quartic-ref-05-near-optimal-separations-of-certificate-co/extracted.md:220–350` and `1873–1963`. Its title and Theorems 2.1–2.3 concern certificate complexity against query measures; it must not be cited as proving the present randomized-versus-quantum separation. The annex labels this as the v2 PDF, dated 17 September 2026; the v3 TeX annex is a separate source. Public retrieval of the versioned v2 abstract and HTML was attempted in this session but returned `DisabledError`, so this note relies on the supplied v2 source for that paper rather than claiming a successful online verification.

## What changes the formal construction account

The existing construction is already fully total at the physical-input level: arbitrary Boolean functions on `BitLoc` are valid inputs. The winner condition itself checks whether the partial-function instances in the proposed winning column are defined, whether their alleged transcripts are true, whether the column is all ones, and whether all its outward pointers select zero bits. Incorrect certificates and unpromised instances are rejected by the conjunction of local tests. This is visible in `Statement.lean`'s input and `WinsAt` definitions and in `Construction/LocalTests.lean:237–355`; it is not an assumption imported from the cheat-sheet literature.

The adaptive-depth qualification is mathematically essential. Reading a pointer word and then its selected target bit takes logarithmically many queries, although different pointer contents can select every target coordinate. The attached paper already says this correctly in Lemma 2.2. The new pointer-schema relevance proof exposes the corrected ledger's support qualification without accusing the paper of an error it does not make. The actual local-test formula is connected to that schema by a definitional equality, and the follow-up physical-support module transports every one-coordinate witness into the full input space. See rows `lem-2.2.i` and `def-column-layout`, `new-r2-pointer-essential-coordinates`, and `new-r2-pointer-physical-support`.

The coherent clause required a stronger formal statement than the old uniform-index distribution. The submitted direct-sum construction quantifies over arbitrary complex amplitudes and preserves each index block, then proves that exactness means no wrong-label amplitude survives. The existing `IsCirc.sigma` rule provides the common read bound and the existing `compile_circ` theorem supplies the physical bit-query bound. The complete theorem quantifies the finite physical embedding for the same operator witnesses. These are proof-source statements, not newly compiled evidence. See `lem-2.2.ii`, `lem-2.2`, `new-r2-exact-law-support`, and `new-r2-arbitrary-index-coherence`.
