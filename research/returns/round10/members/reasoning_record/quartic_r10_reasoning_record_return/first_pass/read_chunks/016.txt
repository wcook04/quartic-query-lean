At history length $h$ the state preparation uses $h$ controlled comparison circuits, while the uniform superposition and the success conjunction are input independent, so the preparation and its inverse each cost at most a constant times $c(h+1)$ queries. Lemma 3.1 makes at most a constant times $\sqrt m\ln(1/\varepsilon)$ calls to these preparations, and its limits on trials and iterations apply even when the success probability is below $1/(2m)$. Summing over $h=0,\ldots,T-1$ and using $\sum_{h=0}^{T-1}(h+1)=T(T+1)/2$ we obtain:

$$
\text{queries}
=O\!\left(c\sqrt m\,T^2\ln(1/\varepsilon)\right)
=O\!\left(c\sqrt m\,T^2\log T\right).
$$

The second equality uses $1/\varepsilon=1000T$ and $T\geq1$ (indeed $T\geq11$ when $m\geq2$), the constants are absolute, and early stopping can only reduce the cost. This proves Proposition 3.2: uniformly for every family of coherently available comparison circuits satisfying the stated cap, one algorithm returns a list of at most $T$ vertices, has the displayed query cap on every input, and contains every specified dominant vertex $s$ with probability at least $1-2m/2^T-T\varepsilon$. No estimate of the $a_{ij}$'s is required.
