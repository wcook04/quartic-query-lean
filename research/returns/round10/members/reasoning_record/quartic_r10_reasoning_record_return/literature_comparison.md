# Comparative literature note: techniques for a complete reasoning record

## Provenance and purpose

This note compares specific passages with the reader tasks in the current reasoning record. It is **secondary synthesis of the panel’s inspected-source records**. The writer of this note read those records, not all the original works independently. “Integrating reviewer,” “quantum reviewer” and “classical reviewer” below identify who inspected the underlying source. Their scope registers govern every claim of access. No new browsing was performed for this note, and the frozen chronological manuscript reading was not changed.

The proposed applications are this review’s reasoning, not recommendations attributed to the cited authors. They concern what a reader can reconstruct: the task of a definition, the hypothesis of a transferred theorem, the state preserved by a procedure, and the evidence behind an exact claim. They do not authorize deleting proofs required by the record’s complete-proof purpose. Source summaries are kept short; the main emphasis is their application and limits.

## 1. Give the introduction one level of technical detail at a time

**Passage and inspection.** John Etnyre, *The Art of Writing Introductions*, Notices AMS 66(3), March 2019, pp. 361–362, DOI [10.1090/noti1816](https://doi.org/10.1090/noti1816). The integrating reviewer read both pages through the [linked article PDF](https://marktomforde.com/academic/gradstudents/AMSarticles/earlycareer/2019/rnoti-p361.pdf). This was an **online source beyond the supplied annex**; register W4.

**Technique.** The introduction establishes the result, its context and its significance at a readable level of precision, with subdivisions serving identifiable reader needs.

**Application and limit.** The record’s opening (line 5) already explains columns and winning. Its next burden is a compressed fixed-time probability argument: allowance, address-vector success, repetition count, union bound and heavy columns. Preserve the planted-zero/cell mechanism and the exponent comparison there; move the exact event to the lower-bound roadmap. This is a choice of location, not a proposal to weaken the argument. Guidance for an ordinary research article cannot determine how much evidence a complete reasoning record must retain.

## 2. Use a running example to expose the next operation

**Passage and inspection.** Francis Edward Su, [*Guidelines for Good Mathematical Writing*](https://math.hmc.edu/su/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2020/08/Guidelines-for-Good-Mathematical-Writing.pdf), updated 18 August 2020, §§1–2, pp. 1–3. The integrating reviewer read the complete supplied text and checked the official PDF text; W1.

**Technique.** The distance-formula example distinguishes givens from the intended conclusion and explains useful transformations. The long-proof guidance makes examples and purposeful section openings practical tools.

**Application and limit.** Across §§4.3, 4.7 and 6.3, let the four-column board recur when a reader encounters a certificate, a rival comparison and a planted modification. Each recurrence should identify the new operation it makes visible, rather than repeat the entire board. The current XOR planting toy has a legitimate simplifying purpose and explicitly translates its labels; it need not be forced into the same numerical Forrelation instance. A running example earns its space through a new check, not through visual consistency alone.

## 3. Attach an invariant or reason to an algorithmic claim

**Passage and inspection.** Knuth, Larrabee and Roberts, [*Mathematical Writing*](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/papers/cs1193.pdf), Stanford report STAN-CS-88-1193, 1988: §1 rules 1–24, §10 pp. 20–22, §§31–33 pp. 69–72, §42 pp. 106–111. The integrating reviewer read these selections and introductory matter, not the whole report; W2. This is not an undifferentiated citation to the later MAA edition.

**Technique.** The inspected discussions connect informal and formal accounts, annotate algorithm steps with invariants, and separate an assertion from its justification.

**Application and limit.** The exact circuit-size identity in §3.4 needs a ledger that ties its summands to the constructors being counted. Similarly, a simulator description should identify the invariant maintained before each proposed query. Neither improvement requires more motivational prose: it requires the missing correspondence between a displayed object and the operation producing it. The report records differing views on stylistic prescriptions, so it does not support treating every local preference as a defect.

## 4. Layer proof obligations without fragmenting the argument

**Passage and inspection.** Leslie Lamport, [*How to Write a 21st Century Proof*](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/proof.pdf), 23 November 2011, revised 15 January 2012; §§1–3 and §§4.1–4.4, printed pp. 1–14, especially the mean-value example and §4.4. The integrating reviewer read these passages online, including Figures 1–5; the later sections and formal appendix were not read. **Beyond-annex source**, W3; DOI [10.1007/s11784-012-0071-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11784-012-0071-6).

**Technique.** Nested justifications expose hypotheses, while an intuitive sketch between a claim and its proof preserves the main sequence. The worked example distinguishes choosing arbitrary points from asserting that suitable distinct points exist.

**Application and limit.** Use this discipline at the planted-cell step in §6.5: state the joint event, construct the capped predictor, establish event inclusion, then invoke the product bound. Each obligation has a different role. Avoid converting every sentence into a numbered proof node; the benefit is visible dependence, not adoption of another formal notation.

## 5. Introduce a representation before its search strategy

**Passage and inspection.** Andris Ambainis, [*Understanding Quantum Algorithms via Query Complexity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06349v1), arXiv:1712.06349v1, 18 December 2017, §4 pp. 9–12 and §5.1 pp. 12–13. The quantum reviewer read these sections, the introduction and §2.1; inspected PDF pages 4 and 10 visually. Later sections were not read through; QS13.

**Technique.** The pointer discussion introduces the board, accepting conditions and certificate consequence before explaining the search. The cheat-sheet discussion separates locating the addressed cell from verifying it.

**Application and limit.** The record can use its existing three-property preview (§3, lines 454–460) as a contract, reach the total construction in §4, and develop the full analytic hardness proof as a later module. This is a sequencing proposal: the preview is already present, so there is no missing-interface finding. The survey’s lower bounds are sketches; their brevity cannot justify omitting the record’s adaptive simulation or probability arguments.

## 6. Explain a register map by its input, output and retained information

**Passage and inspection.** Andrew M. Childs, [*Lecture Notes on Quantum Algorithms*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~amchilds/qa/qa.pdf), supplied title-page edition 17 April 2025: §1.4, printed pp. 2–3; §§19.1–19.3, pp. 87–89; §§21.1–21.3, pp. 97–99. The quantum reviewer read these sections and the preface, not the book; PDF pages 10–11 were visually inspected. The unversioned URL does not identify immutable bytes; the register records the supplied hash, QS12.

**Technique.** Reversible computation is presented through an explicit map and the reason intermediate registers are retained or cleaned. Search analysis separates the task, mathematical estimate and implementation cost.

**Application and limit.** In §2.5, explain entry and oracle-compatible embeddings through the jobs their different invariants perform. For amplification, separately state the successful output law, retained state and all-input query cap. Classical compute-copy-uncompute does not automatically justify cleaning a bounded-error quantum sampler while preserving its conditional successful state.

## 7. Keep two Forrelation experiments distinct

**Passage and inspection.** Aaronson–Ambainis, [*Forrelation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5729v1), arXiv:1411.5729v1, 21 November 2014, §3.2, Proposition 6 and adjacent derivation, PDF pp. 13–14: read by the quantum reviewer with bounded §3.1 context, QS02. Compare Bansal–Sinha, [arXiv:2008.07003v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07003v3), Proposition 1.2 and Corollary 1.4, PDF pp. 3–4; statement/quantum-consequence reading by that reviewer, QS03. The classical reviewer inspected wider passages in CLS-S02.

**Technique.** The direct circuit first makes the Forrelation scalar observable; the controlled two-branch improvement then produces a different acceptance probability. The two tests have different dependence on the promise gap.

**Application and limit.** In §3.3 and its §7.3 use, give each experiment its own probability, threshold and repetition calculation. A squared-amplitude test cannot inherit the linear test’s uniform dependence on $k$ merely because both solve the fixed-$k$ promise problem. The simpler test may be sufficient for fixed parameters; the growing-parameter conclusion makes the distinction consequential. The quantum reviewer did not independently match all local Bansal–Sinha bytes to the online edition.

## 8. Separate successful-state preservation from a deterministic stopping cap

**Passage and inspection.** Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp, [*Quantum Amplitude Amplification and Estimation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055v1), v1, 15 May 2000, §2, Lemma 1, Theorems 2–3 and the beginning of §2.1 through Theorem 4; read by the quantum reviewer, QS04. Boyer–Brassard–Høyer–Tapp, [*Tight bounds on quantum searching*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034v1), v1, 23 May 1996, Lemmas 1–2 and §4, PDF pp. 3–4; inspected by the same reviewer, QS05. Later journal editions were not substituted.

**Technique.** The invariant-plane calculation identifies the unchanged normalized good component. The random-iteration averaging identity addresses success probability; unknown-success search and its stopping rule are separate matters.

**Application and limit.** Present §5.1’s amplification contract in two clauses: exact successful output behavior and bounded cost/failure. Then make clear which clause the weighted sampler uses. Expected-cost search, including nontermination at zero success, is not the all-input deterministic cap needed here. The record must retain its separate capped construction.

## 9. Distinguish stored history from an implicit search state

**Passage and inspection.** Mande–Paraashar–Saurabh, [*Randomized and Quantum Query Complexities of Finding a King in a Tournament*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2023.30), published FSTTCS 2023 version, LIPIcs 284: opening quantum overview, pp. 30:3–30:4, and §4, pp. 30:9–30:13. The quantum reviewer read the main construction and accounting, but not the appendix proofs of Lemmas 17–18; QS09.

**Technique.** The algorithm stores a small history while representing the surviving tournament implicitly. Membership, sampling and estimation interfaces precede their use by the main routine.

**Application and limit.** At §5.2’s entry, state once that the pivot list is stored, while $w_i$ and total weight are analysis quantities. Explain how coherent comparisons generate their law without computing a weight table. This prevents both an unnecessary charge for enumerating weights and an unjustified assumption of direct weight access. The source samples uniformly from a fixed feasible set with exact edges; its theorem does not establish the record’s fractional comparison law.

## 10. Present the capped specialization before a more general direct-product theorem

**Passage and inspection.** Andrew Drucker, [*Improved Direct Product Theorems for Randomized Query Complexity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.0644v2), arXiv:1005.0644v2, 9 May 2014: §§1.1–1.3, relevant §2 definitions, §3 including Lemmas 3.1–3.2 and the final fair-algorithm remark, and §5 Lemma 5.1; read by the classical reviewer, CLS-S01. For comparison, that reviewer read Shaltiel’s supplied author/preprint §5, Definition 4, Theorem 5 and Lemma 7, pp. 11–12; its [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00037-003-0175-x) does not establish byte identity, CLS-S03.

**Technique.** The potential is introduced by the prediction problem it solves. The fair case is distinguished from unrestricted allocation; fairness is a pathwise per-instance cap, not an average.

**Application and limit.** Keep §6.1’s separate-cap result, transcript factorization and §6.5’s capped simulator on the main proof path. Explain the global-budget alternative afterward. Shaltiel’s XOR-advantage statement is not the vector-output conclusion, and conditioning on a light-column event is not licensed by transcript factorization. Neither source removes the need to prove the present simulator’s independence conditions.

## 11. Separate an inspected source defect from a theorem’s overall status

## 12. Make a transfer lemma carry the changed assumptions

**Passage and inspection.** Girish–Tal–Wu, [*Fourier Growth of Parity Decision Trees*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11604v2), v2, 13 May 2021, §1.3, pp. 6–8, Lemma 1.9 and the truncation footnote: inspected by the classical reviewer after the primary freeze, CLS-S10, not the complete parity proof. **Technique.** The first passage distinguishes stopping at a bad event from conditioning away that event. The second immediately extends a Fourier bound to real leaf labels by averaging.

**Application and limit.** In §3.12 and Appendix E, put the centered variables, stopping rule and biased-derivative transfer beside the equations they authorize. For the optional Fourier comparison, show the monomial restriction identity before its analytic payoff. The sources do not directly supply the record’s biased constant, a certified numerical Fourier constant, or a transfer of rorrelation distributions to the Walsh promise.

## 14. Use newer direct-product work to sharpen the output and cost contract

**Passage and inspection.** Ben-David–Blais, [*Direct Product Theorems for Randomized Query Complexity*](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08268v1), arXiv:2512.08268v1, 9 December 2025: §1.1 definitions/Theorem 1 and §1.2 list-decoding definition/Theorem 2. **Technique.** The introduction distinguishes worst-case and expected-cost notions before stating its direct-product results; list decoding makes an answer-vector-containing list an explicit output object.

**Application and limit.** This helps explain §6.5’s visited-address viewpoint and why the record nevertheless uses a particular capped predictor. State cost, success range and output type before any proposed substitution. This limited theorem/interface reading supports comparison of contracts, not replacement of the record’s proof or a claim that the newer result automatically strengthens it.

## 15. Compare tournament results by oracle promise

**Passage and inspection.** Arora–Kumar, [*Quantum Query Complexity of Tournament Solutions*](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39702v1), v1, 29 October 2026: abstract, introduction, §2.1 and §6.1 upper-bound argument, inspected online by the quantum reviewer; lower bounds were not read completely, QS10. Both are **beyond-annex sources**.

The record allows arbitrary pairwise probabilities away from a genuine winner; an exact-edge or bounded-error-bit theorem does not automatically cover that setting. These sources support current terminology and a bounded comparison, not a claim that their algorithms eliminate the fractional-weight analysis.
