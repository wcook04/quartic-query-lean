## 1. Research mathematician in analysis or combinatorics

**Background.** Finite-dimensional linear algebra, elementary conditioning, asymptotic quantifiers and proof by induction; no course in quantum computing or query complexity. This is the primary perspective whose record is frozen in `first_pass/`.

**First demand that could not yet be explained from the preceding text.** At U0011, the optimal power for total functions is announced before the definitions of totality and the complexity measures. This is an ordinary announcement and is resolved by lines 18–26. The more consequential first unresolved operation is the circuit transcript at U0154: the reader has a named object but cannot yet reconstruct how it can be checked without recomputation. Lines 145–157 later supply that operation. Neither is an established mathematical omission. The architectural cost is that the fully usable four-column object is postponed until PDF page 6 while the opening and general construction already invoke exact constants and future reductions.

**Simulated explanation after the pass.** “A total Boolean function has to work even on the misleading inputs that the hidden partial problem would be allowed to ignore. The stored circuit computations therefore certify both the promise and its answer. Quantumly, a genuine winning column is almost certain to beat any rival, although two losers can compare arbitrarily. A weighted pivot process loses expected total mass geometrically, while a still-unlisted winner retains mass. Its quantum sampler preserves the conditional pivot distribution, and verification of the short list handles false winners. Classically, dummy and planted boards differ in a random column's erased zero and its hidden true certificate cell. A light visit to that cell yields a capped prediction of many independent hard answers. That gives roughly m times the partial hardness, against quantum square-root m. Fixing k after a proposed exponent, then growing m, excludes every exponent below four. The universal quartic upper bound is an imported theorem.”

**Diagnostic limit.** This explanation is informed by the whole paper, and the combined rubric was partly exposed in the same tool batch as its question. It is not an unaided post-test.

## 2. Strong final-year mathematics/computer-science undergraduate

**Background.** Probability, linear algebra, basic algorithms and asymptotic notation; little complexity theory and no prior quantum course. **Range and order:** all PDF pages 1–24, in order, after the primary source pass; all figure pixels and both probability/trace tables were inspected. This is a fully informed reread, not a new encounter.

**First point requiring unsupported forward vocabulary.** At line 129/page 5, the terms hard distribution, joint capped event and stopped simulator appear before the running board has been instantiated. The reader can trust the sentence, but cannot yet use it. The later proof resolves it, so the recommendation is to relocate this quantitative explanation of r, not to claim that a missing proof must be added. A second transition is the beginning of Section 4: a query-cost rule for nested search is used to motivate the tournament before the amplification operation is taught. That is usable as an announced contract, but the presentation would be easier to reconstruct if the cost contract and its later derivation were visibly separated.

**Simulated explanation.** “The data include a secret address and a certificate at that address. A real winner has only ones in its own string and can point to a zero in every other string. A fake certificate can fool the address decoder, so checking the domain output matters. Comparisons make a shortlist cheaply; the shortlist, rather than every column, gets the expensive checks. The pivot weights are survival probabilities, not probabilities of being a winner, and they are sampled in proportion to their sizes. In the classical argument, writing down the cell coordinate is already guessing the whole secret address. The simulator stops before it would ask too much about that address, so its success can be bounded without conditioning on a lucky cheap run. For k=4 the displayed estimates exclude powers below 3.5; k=5 is needed to exclude 3.5 itself.”

**What this tests.** It distinguishes cell labels from stored pointers; probability masses from amplitudes; global budget from forwarded-query cap; and an endpoint from a limit. It does not show that an unassisted student will make these distinctions.

## 3. Physicist or engineer with a first course in quantum circuits

**Background.** Unitary gates, tensor products, measurement and reversible computation; elementary probability; little theoretical computer science. **Targeted reconsideration of already-read Markdown:** lines 14–51, 76–102, 114–167, 177–204, 208–341, 350–400 and 466–494, organized in that order after the PDF reread; the material was already available in context. Answers and the construction are already known.

**First conceptual obstacle specific to this reader.** The exact-versus-promised certification task at lines 114–145 is less familiar than the circuit algebra. A circuit-trained reader may instinctively ask for a practical gate-cost advantage, but the introductory oracle-cost convention expressly excludes that interpretation. The theorem's nontrivial content is the comparison of access budgets on the same encoding, including dishonest inputs. Once this is accepted, the retained-workspace argument is directly usable. There is no reason to require clean output registers: the normalized good state includes all the workspace, and the full preparation has an inverse.

**Simulated explanation.** “The relevant resource is calls to a bit oracle. The sign experiment produces a signed overlap, while its projection probability is the square, which is why negative off-promise inputs can imitate positive ones. The certificate's domain output repairs that problem in the total task. A controlled preparation keeps the comparison work registers, so their squared norms implement the same stochastic comparison law. Amplification rotates the good and bad components of the entire state; it changes total success probability but leaves the successful label distribution intact. The algorithm can therefore sample an analytically defined pivot law without estimating its weights. This does not imply a runtime advantage or a way to read every bit from a superposition.”

**Revision implication.** Make the probabilistic comparison contract and ideal process a continuous block; place the retained-register realization immediately before the controlled sampler that uses it. Preserve the physical oracle model and full-workspace inverse, but do not send an already circuit-literate reader through an unrelated remediation sequence.

## 4. Lecturer planning an advanced undergraduate/beginning graduate course

**Background.** Able to teach all the supplied mathematics; assessing prerequisites, sequencing and tasks, not pretending to be a fourth novice. **Targeted reconsideration of already-read material:** all section openings; exercise prompts at lines 98, 167, 315, 377, 419, 482 and 486; their answers at 498–512; optional-route instructions at 47–51 and 438–453; the formal extension at 488–494. PDF pages 7–8, 11–14, 17–22 were reconsidered from the already viewed images.

**Course decision.** Use the article as a source for three connected teaching modules, with the exact estimates as optional reading or a later exercise. This is a proposed allocation, not an empirically measured teaching duration.

1. **Promises and verification.** The four-bit oracle convention, sign experiment, base board, domain-bit variant and local equations. End with Question 1 and a short student explanation of why a false transcript has a first inconsistent gate. Students need not reproduce a 180-gate truth-table synthesis.
2. **Comparisons and quantum sampling.** False-pointer and cycle variants; the ideal weight process; its fractional example; controlled preparation; amplification law preservation; permanent list and verification. End with Question 2 and a separate task that links persistent winner mass to the missing-winner probability. The averaged-trigonometric constant can be an explicit accepted input.
3. **Adaptive lower bounds and the exponent.** Independent planting, the two changed regions, heavy/light decomposition, stopped simulator, then the final gap and fixed-k ratio. Questions 3–5 test independence, whole-run caps and endpoints. Assign the optional direct-product proof to students ready for conditional product laws. A retargeted final synthesis task should make students derive the three charges and the mL scale rather than only execute the numerical examples.

**Concrete changes needed for independent course use.** Put each board variant's starting state, changed data and intended task in a compact reference table. Name the cycled board at its later uses. Give the quantum routine a contract before teaching its implementation. Place the three classical charge terms beside the events they bound. Put the final combined rubric after the numbered answers and label it guided feedback. Separate the optional Lean statement task from historical build chronology. Keep the final fixed-alpha/fixed-k argument on the main route.

**Simulated explanation to a class.** “We are combining three operations: hide a verifiable certificate at a hard address; find a candidate through comparisons without checking everyone; and show that a cheap classical visit would predict the address. Each exercise should test one of those operations under a changed condition. The theorem is about all-input bounded-error query budgets, and its final quantifiers are as much part of the result as the pictures.”

**Assessment caution.** All later responses are answer-exposed. The report proposes acceptance tasks; it does not report completed student trials, reader success rates or improved learning.
