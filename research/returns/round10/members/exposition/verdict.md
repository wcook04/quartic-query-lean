## A coherent revision order

First decide where the full family theorem lives and bring the base board to the first place that needs a usable winning candidate. Keep the introductory access model, total-versus-promised distinction, credit and qualitative endpoint. Define general parameters before their first symbolic use; moving the theorem is not permission to leave the earlier local-test count undefined.

Next reorganize Section 4 as one dependency sequence. The comparison probabilities must first be the law of the actual measured experiment. The ideal process consumes that law. The coherent preparation must reproduce it, and the amplification explanation must preserve its exact successful-label distribution through mixed iteration counts and first-success retries. The every-input cap, $q=0,1$ endpoints and history condition before a winner is listed stay on the main route. Only the numerical schedule derivation becomes an optional inspection layer.

Then reorganize the classical proof after the paired inputs and first-difference event are defined. Give each charge an event and a budget interpretation: erased selected zero, heavily queried selected address tuple, and light true-cell visit. These charges can overlap. Let the final combined task ask a classical-route reader to derive their sum and use $G\le c\,mL$, $L\le m$ and $(2/3)^r\le m^{-10}$. A quantum-only route may continue to accept the classical bound as stated.

Finally normalize the recurring examples, the answers and the formal evidence note. Inspect the revised PDF, not only the source. An internal link that lands on a shared answer page can still expose the synthesis even after the textual order improves.

## What would distinguish a serviceable next edition from an excellent one

A serviceable revision would make all local references unambiguous, keep the mathematics recoverable and place links and answers sensibly. An excellent revision would make the algorithmic choices feel motivated at their first substantial use. The reader would know why comparisons are the cheap operation, why their arbitrary loser behavior still permits progress, why the successful-label law rather than merely a success probability matters, and why naming a cell is already predicting an address.

Use those operations as acceptance tasks. A construction-route reader should justify all four winning conditions and the need for domain outputs. A quantum-route reader should derive the conditional law from fresh registers and explain why a permanent list survives later failure. A classical-route reader should derive the three charges without conditioning on lightness, then perform the fixed-$\alpha$, fixed-$k$, growing-$m$ argument. These are proposed tests of the revised text. This review does not claim that learners have passed them.
