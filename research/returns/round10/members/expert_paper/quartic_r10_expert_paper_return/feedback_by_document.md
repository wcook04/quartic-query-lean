## 3. Quantum proof: center the exact distribution

The decisive local object is not a generic search subroutine. It is the relation between a fresh-register preparation and the current surviving weights. With \(w_i\) the product of the comparison probabilities against the selected pivots, the preparation has success-and-label mass \(w_i/m\). Its success probability is \(W/m\), where \(W=\sum_i w_i\); conditioned on success, the label distribution is \(w_i/W\). Complementarity between each pair, together with the zero diagonal, gives the ideal halving calculation. These are the construction-specific facts that explain why amplification must preserve a distribution exactly. [Lead §3.2, equations (8)–(9); Q-S03; evidence/quantum_verification_checks.json.]

## 4. Classical proof: keep the law, the event and the two clocks together

The main lower-bound route has three contributions to its discrepancy bound:

| Term | Event or resource controlled | Relevant reason |
|---|---|---|
| \(4G/m^2\) | Discovering the erased zero | Independent planted index and adaptive control of zero discoveries in the dummy run. |
| \(G/(mL)\) | Selecting a column whose terminal target-query count exceeds \(L\) | Total budget averaged over the independently chosen planted column. |
| \(G\beta^r\) | Hitting the planted address while the column is light | A capped predictor for each fixed column/time, followed by a time union and cancellation of the column average. |

This table describes the inspected proof, not a proposal to change its bound. The product lemma controls prediction of all \(r\) answers under the original hard law. The simulator knows jointly independent outside data, stops before a forbidden target query, and predicts the address when the original algorithm first requests a candidate cell coordinate. That point is before the cell's contents are returned. On the original-law event that the target remains light and the queried cell is the true cell, the capped predictor agrees with the original run. This event inclusion is what permits the direct-product estimate. It does not require replacing the input law by its conditional distribution given lightness. [Lead §§4.1–4.5; CL-current-record; CL-original-preprint; specialist_notes/classical_verification.md.]
