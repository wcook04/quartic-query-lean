# Verdict

The separation's detailed classical mechanism survives this review. The hard law, jointly independent side information, enforced simulator cap, joint-event estimate and final budget argument are recoverable and correctly joined in the reviewed proof. The most consequential defects are statements around that proof, several exercise answers, and the exposition's mismatch with its assigned audience. No core theorem refutation was found. This is a reading assessment, not a new formal-verification result.

## The ten changes that matter most

1. **Restore the probability statement's time and event scope.** The record opening drops the fixed-query-time restriction and the all-time union factor. The exposition's zero-discovery argument must condition on the preceding transcript, not a completed run. The full simulator proof already has the right conditions. LS114; LS093; LS036.

2. **Make the exercises say exactly what their arguments prove.** Fixed caps are not fixed realized counts. A bounded-error verifier need not certainly reject the losing toy candidates. For the stated goal α_tot≥3.5, k=4 suffices by excluding every smaller exponent; k=5 is a valid choice of the displayed stronger recipe for excluding the endpoint itself. LS088; LS112; LS110.

3. **Correct the pointer-function antecedent.** ABBLSS require the designated highlighted zero to point back, not every zero. Explain the earlier direct-pointer objection through the access cost that the present construction changes. LS012; LS070; LS153.

4. **Repair the existing reading routes and stale scope statements.** Several visible section labels now send the reader to the wrong companion argument. Appendix B still denies a growing-k schedule after §7.3 supplies one. A working file link does not repair either error. LS118; LS161; LS155; LS201; LS203.

5. **Keep the assigned newcomer as the exposition's audience.** A newly declared graduate prerequisite list does not fulfill the requested teaching task. Teach the few operations the worked examples consume and state the deeper imported results precisely. LS063; LS059; LS077.

6. **Explain the off-promise acceptance bridge in the companion.** The two laws are correctly distinguished, but “Because the promise is one-sided” does not replace the argument using moments, pointwise acceptance bounds and reduced error. LS047; LS137; LS138.

7. **Expose the classical reduction's accounting.** Separate the simulator definition from event containment; show the average over the selected column; teach a stopped and an unstopped predictor run using the existing XOR example. LS039; LS042; LS099; LS100; LS102.

8. **Show where the Forrelation exponent and k losses come from.** Explain the d^(k/2)m^−(k−1)/2 balance and the two k factors before detailed constants. A low-degree contracted-array example would clarify the hardest proof interface. LS134; LS136; LS139.

9. **Reallocate space from unused arithmetic to those transitions.** Keep complete counts in the record. Make the growing-k refinement optional and distinguish its validity threshold from an informative finite exponent. LS026; LS051; LS052; LS084; LS193.

10. **Make the finite pictures and local tests exact.** Expert Figure 2 says m=4 while drawing eight bits per string. The exposition's board is correct. A single explicit gate check would teach more than the unexplained 180-gate and 371-test totals. LS034; LS073; LS072; LS075.
