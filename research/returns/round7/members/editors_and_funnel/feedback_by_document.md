## Expert companion: ready for another editorial pass

The mathematical core is ready for a knowledgeable reader to engage with, but the companion warrants a substantial prose and weighting revision before it is presented as a finished specialist account. Its strongest passages explain the cheap comparison against an arbitrary opponent, the replacement of a surviving set by probability weights, the exact sampling law, and the capped predictor for the planted address. Those passages show how the theorem works. The surrounding apparatus often gives routine constants, provenance comparisons and formal correspondence the same prominence as those ideas.

My editorial verdict is therefore to retain the construction and the two central analyses, while recasting the opening and ending around a clearer reading purpose. I would read the paper to the end as a referee assigned to this result. As an editor deciding how to present it, I would first ask for a sharper account of the companion's own contribution and a more economical route to it. This is a request for better selection and explanation of existing material. It does not ask for a stronger theorem, a new separation or another formalisation project.

### What the reader currently takes away

A specialist will learn that the exponent four is optimal for total Boolean functions and will probably recover the right high-level arithmetic: a hard address costs a classical algorithm about m^(1−1/k) per relevant column, the hidden column contributes m, and the quantum search over columns costs roughly sqrt(m). The reader will also see that totality is maintained through exact local certification rather than by assuming every address block is promised.

That intended message competes with a second one: this is an extensively checked repository containing many explicit constants and multiple formal routes. The introduction lists the constants before explaining the construction. The final section devotes more than a printed page to formal endpoint details, two universal upper-bound constants, empty-infimum conventions, auxiliary amplifier forms and the direct-product cap predicate. Each fact has a reason to exist somewhere. Their combined weight makes the companion resemble a compressed account of the whole project.

The reader should instead leave able to explain two exact changes to the predecessor machinery. First, each candidate column has its own hidden certificate, so a classical algorithm cannot satisfy a single cheap address computation and finish; the lower bound controls the chance of reaching a randomly planted column's useful cell. Second, the quantum algorithm can cheaply compare candidates while relying only on the genuine winner's behaviour. Probability weights implement the shrinking-candidate idea even when the losers have no definite orientation. Verification then turns a short candidate list into a total decision procedure. These ideas deserve the paper's most legible sentences, its diagrams and its strongest transitions.

### The opening needs a contribution hierarchy

The title omits the total-function setting. That restriction should be visible even when the title is separated from the abstract. The subtitle and immediate attribution to OpenAI are good and should remain. The abstract then needs to introduce k before using it to explain 4−2/k, and its classical lower-bound wording needs a probability or hard-distribution qualification. No lower bound says every successful execution must pay the stated cost on every positive input.

The phrase “complete argument” is also too broad for what this companion prints. A conventional specialist paper can certainly cite substantial inputs. The problem is the gap between the initial promise and the later explanation that several construction and Forrelation ingredients, the universal bound, and the explicit uniform estimate are imported. Describe the actual contribution at the first promise of scope, and keep the precise citations at their mathematical uses.

The first page then contains the full definition of alpha_tot, the theorem, the earlier universal bound and conjecture, and a long contribution paragraph whose leading item is an explicit-constant ledger. The formula defining alpha_tot is useful, but the question should motivate it. The numerical values belong with the bounds that use them. Repeatedly showing that the same circuit supplies both estimates is important only to the extent that it protects the combined claim; it need not occupy the opening before the reader has met the circuit's role.

The historical discussion has improved considerably. The AND–OR cost calculation explains why that named recipe gives exponent 2+c and stops at three. That is much better than a chronology by itself, and the current text correctly avoids asserting an impossibility theorem for all cheat sheets. The table, however, should use a lower-bound relation for the separating examples and distinguish them from the universal upper bound. Its current equality heading gives weaker and stronger quantifier directions the same appearance.

The lineage discussion then spreads over several algorithmic families. The back-pointer paragraph needs clearer names for the two variants it compares. The comparison-search paragraph should foreground the closest surviving-candidate antecedent and the present probability-weighted change. The other distinctions can live next to the proposition they help a reader assess. Finally, the introduction's closing paragraph combines a roadmap, lists of imported arguments, a rule about every other record link, and an exposition pointer. Those are separate functions. A short mathematical route should lead directly into the construction.

### The construction is mostly well explained, with one concrete figure error

The exact domain/value circuit and the local-test dichotomy are well chosen for this account. In particular, the sentence that every address of a non-winner has a failing local test is essential. It is the reason a decoder can behave arbitrarily off the promise without compromising the decision algorithm. Keep that quantifier intact.

Several small wording choices make the construction harder than it needs to be. The overview defines the true address before qualifying it by the promise. The phrase “independent input bits,” repeated visually as “independent instances” in Figure 1, mixes separately specified coordinates with probabilistic independence. The uniqueness argument says a pointer into a second all-one column “would find a zero there,” leaving the contradiction implicit. The exact cell and test counts need a stated convention for the circuit size S. These are low-cost edits that remove avoidable ambiguity.

The optional conversion to an input-length exponent, 1/44+o(1), is a digression. Its mathematical content may be useful in the record, but the main argument relates R to Q, and the next necessary idea is local verification. The conversion also relies on the lower polynomial scale of n implicit in the exact size formula; it does not follow merely from the displayed upper estimate on n. A reader should not be asked to work out that inversion here.

Figure 2 contains a literal finite-instance error. Its caption says m=4, but each X_i has eight drawn coordinates, with some zeros at positions five and seven. The source drawing confirms the eight-coordinate loop. Since the definition requires X_i in {0,1}^m, the picture cannot be the claimed four-column, four-bit instance. This is a correctable illustration error, not a counterexample to the theorem. The two coupled panels should also make shared address data visually explicit; separate boxes bearing the same sampling law can suggest independent copies even though the caption correctly says they are identical.

### The tournament proof is the centre to preserve

The quantum section's central mechanism is strong. The canonical comparison circuit, retained label registers, probability identity a_ij+a_ji=1, and winner-only success guarantee explain why arbitrary loser comparisons cause no problem. The move from a surviving set to weights is both informative and properly connected to an antecedent.

The proof then delivers what the explanation promises. Pairing terms yields the expected-weight estimate, the winner retains enough weight until selected, and the displayed preparation gives success-and-label mass w_i/m. The statement that the algorithm never computes the weights is particularly effective: it answers the practical objection exactly where it arises. The stopped, history-dependent coupling is also worth protecting. A casual global conditioning argument would lose a real hypothesis; the present wording keeps the right conditional law.

The main opportunities are to remove routine material surrounding this proof and to clarify a few local expressions. The printed amplification lemma includes a returned-state guarantee stronger than the main route needs. The distinction from the measured formal procedure is now stated accurately, but the account could avoid carrying the unused strength through the main paper. The proof sketch still gives the numerical rotation and trial calculation in considerable detail. Its necessary lesson is a fixed cap with a constant-success trial and the exact conditional label law.

Likewise, the deliberately loose logarithmic count should be much shorter. It is useful to know that the overall cost is sqrt(m) times a fixed power of log(m). It is less useful to read repeated padding, trial and iteration factors after being told no later conclusion depends on that power. The existing record is the proper destination for the exact bookkeeping.

Verification needs careful vocabulary. A failing test is a defect; a search that fails to find such a test leads to acceptance. Calling both outcomes failure creates an avoidable inversion. The error formula should also identify why zero-inputs incur a union over T candidate acceptances whereas a positive input only needs the listed winner's fresh decoding to work.

### The lower-bound reduction deserves breathing room

The separate-cap proof using unnormalised measures is an excellent choice. Its opening explains why that formalism is used, and the recurrence tracks the single factor affected by an adaptive query. The revised statement that Drucker's global-budget theorem would also suffice is accurate and valuable. It should not be lost in a future effort to simplify the literature discussion.

The coupled dummy and planted inputs are also a productive organisation. There are exactly two kinds of changed location: one matrix bit and one certificate block. Say that explicitly instead of “two places.” The drawing, its caption and inequality (10) should make the same distinction.

The erased-zero estimate needs one small scope clarification: conditional uniformity on unqueried rows is asserted only before the first discovery of that column's zero. Repeated queries to an already found zero do not create discoveries. The optional sharper probability bound should either name its quantity or leave the main proof entirely.

The predictor in §4.4 is the other passage to protect. Query position names the proposed address before any cell value is used; free data are jointly independent of the target address instances; truncation produces a capped algorithm; and the useful event is a joint event under the original law. These are the reasons the reduction works. The paragraph is simply doing too much at once. Separate the predictor's construction from the event inclusion and state its output on abort explicitly. One visible averaging step would also show why the sum over columns produces G beta^r rather than mG beta^r.

The end should emphasise the discrepancy contradiction and the general mL lower bound. Floor arithmetic and the optional numerical refinement can be subordinate. They should not receive the final rhetorical weight.

### The final sections need a cleaner stopping point

The fixed-k ratio argument in §6 is precise and sufficient for alpha_tot=4. The growing-k family is an optional further perspective. Its derivation now has a proper record destination, so the companion can state its purpose and asymptotic conclusion without reproducing the long epsilon formula. The caveat about failing to establish an Omega(Q^4) separation should appear once after both regimes.

The formal section should retain the common-circuit quantifiers, the existential logarithmic exponent in the principal endpoint, the separate source for the explicit constants, and readable links. Those are mathematical correspondence facts a specialist may actually need. Repeating the initial model conventions, comparing two unused universal constants and explaining the full auxiliary-lemma correspondence makes the section disproportionately long. The wording about no hypotheses implying complete proofs should also be scoped more carefully: the preceding account of proof checking and axioms supplies the verification fact.

The counterexample table earns its place. It makes assumptions memorable and provides useful audit destinations. It needs row numbers because the body cites rows by number. Its beta description should say upper bound rather than best success, and the zero-query independence example should be identified as a toy illustration beyond the L>=1 setting used in the lower-bound section. Finally, the PDF should keep probability-space qualifiers attached to the inequalities they qualify; a floating figure currently separates one such qualifier onto the next page.

The revision should preserve the existing gains: precise credit, the limited AND–OR barrier, winner-only comparison reliability, all-address verification, exact label sampling, stopped coupling, the original-law cap event, and the fixed-k quantifier order. The remaining work is chiefly the craft of putting those ideas in the foreground and making the surrounding sentences earn their space.


## Exposition: stronger examples, unresolved entry level

### Verdict and the intended reader

I would read this exposition to the end as a mathematically prepared reader who already knows quantum query algorithms. I would assign selected sections to a graduate quantum-algorithms or lower-bounds course after correcting the local mistakes described below. I would not yet assign it without support to the broader newcomer described in the packet. The difficulty is no longer that the paper lacks examples. It now has several excellent ones. The difficulty is that the examples sometimes arrive after the unfamiliar operation they should teach, while standard numerical machinery receives more explanation than the new reduction.

The current reader will probably remember a winning column, an addressed cell, a nontransitive tournament and a planted pair. Those are the right objects. The reader is less likely to reconstruct why a coherent preparation has exactly the required pivot law, why a proposed cell coordinate is already a simultaneous prediction, or how the three different query budgets fit together without conditioning on a favorable run. Those are the operations the exposition should make independently reproducible.

The revised prerequisite paragraph is honest. That is a real improvement over claiming that knowing the query-model definitions is enough. But the packet fixes a broad audience: a strong mathematics or computer-science undergraduate, a mathematician in another area, or a physicist with some quantum computing. Requiring unknown-success amplification, reversible uncomputation, minimax and adaptive decision-tree conditioning cannot by itself complete that teaching task. The needed repairs are small and local rather than a whole introductory textbook: an ordinary query trace, the phase-kickback identity, the operational meaning of the projection, an amplification contract and a short simulator trace. The packet's own `record-to-expository` skill explicitly says that a prerequisite list is not a prerequisite ladder and that silently raising a fixed audience's prerequisites changes the task.

### Correct the local mathematics before revising the style

There are two definite mathematical misstatements in teaching material and one definite description error about the algorithm's resource cap. None refutes the main theorem or the displayed asymptotic ratio argument.

First, §5 says that at `m=4` there is no hard distribution. That is false for the zero-query threshold stated in §1. Put equal probability on the verified promised inputs `a+` and `a0`. A deterministic algorithm making no queries must output a constant and therefore succeeds with probability one half. It satisfies the stated hardness condition of success at most two thirds. The correct limitation is that the toy board does not exhibit the useful asymptotic regime, and it also replaces the production address length by `r=2`. The picture can illustrate the changed locations perfectly well without denying the existence of every hard distribution.

Second, Question 4 asks for a parameter proving that the infimum of universal exponents is at least `3.5`. Its answer says that `k=5` is needed and `k=4` does not suffice. The current bounds at fixed `k=4` already make the ratio diverge for every exponent strictly below `3.5`, since `7/4−α/2>0`. That proves the lower bound on the infimum. A value such as `k=5` is needed for the stronger goal of excluding a universal bound at exponent `3.5` itself by this strict-gap calculation. The question and answer must agree about which goal is being tested. This is especially consequential because the exercise belongs to the section teaching quantifier order.

Third, §4 says the number of preparations is fixed in advance, then repeats that claim for trials, iterations, stages and comparisons. The procedure explicitly randomizes an iteration count, stops trials at first success and stops the tournament on reported failure. The actual counts vary. Their upper caps are fixed in advance. The worst-case query analysis is sound with that distinction, and it does not require adding a padded implementation.

Two other passages deserve precision without being promoted to theorem errors. The erased-zero hazard should condition on the transcript prefix before a query, not suggest conditioning on the completed future transcript. Answer 2 should say that losing candidates have failing string tests; the bounded-error verifier need not discover those failures with certainty. Also, an amplified normalized vector can differ by a global phase, so the exact invariant needed by the argument is the conditional state ray or measured-label law.

### Rebalance the opening and the mechanism

The rendered introduction occupies almost two pages. It asks the reader to absorb the access model, an infimum, the refined theorem, a mechanism sketch, the entire historical progression, the old AND-OR arithmetic, prerequisites, outcomes and course routes before reaching the first concrete input. Most of those components belong somewhere, but their accumulation weakens the examples-first promise.

Keep the question, exact theorem scope and exponent mechanism early. The historical material should then explain the objects as they appear: addressed cells inherit the cheat-sheet idea, outward pointers disqualify competing columns, and the new tournament postpones expensive verification. The exact constants `6` and `1/400` can remain in the theorem without receiving a second explanatory spotlight before the reader understands the powers of `m`. The long approximate-equality chain should be identified as bookkeeping for a lower bound on `R` and an upper bound on `Q`; it is not a pair of matching complexity estimates.

The first use of certificate needs particular care. In query complexity it means a set of input-bit assignments forcing the answer. Here a cell stores witness data whose validation reads address inputs, its own column's string and bits in other columns. Saying that the certificate sits in a cell can blur the difference. That same ambiguity affects the later claim that AND-OR has `n`-bit certificates. A tiny explanation of its two certificate cases would do more for the old-versus-new comparison than another historical exponent.

### Keep the successful examples and make them do more work

The three eight-bit inputs are the exposition's strongest start. The bit/sign convention, explicit Walsh matrix and shared second block make the arithmetic checkable. The negative example is not decoration: it later demonstrates why a correct circuit evaluation needs a domain bit. The newly added one-input with projection success probability one quarter successfully explains why the experiment must be repeated. These repairs should survive any shortening.

The four-column board is equally valuable once its fields are understood. The sentence that every assignment is allowed establishes totality plainly. The changed board shows that true gate evaluations do not imply promise membership. The conclusion that every nonwinner fails a test at every address is the exact invariant needed when a decoder behaves arbitrarily off the promise. Question 1 actually exercises it.

There remain avoidable first-use stalls. `D_z` appears before its definition. Projecting onto `u` is not connected to a measurable outcome. The circuit transcript is declared correct before the object is explained. A concrete pointer test is now present, as round six requested, but the corresponding concrete gate test is still absent. The four winning conditions and four test families deserve a stable, consultable layout because later reasoning repeatedly refers back to them.

The exact 180-gate count and 371-test total are not inherently undesirable. Their value is as optional consistency checks on a finite board. They should not displace the one gate equation that would teach the actual operation. Similarly, the 256-input census is interesting but does not presently advance the proof. It can become an optional counting task or leave the main route.

### The quantum section: move the operation before its supplier details

The section has the right conceptual progression: nested search costs too much; a comparison only needs pointers from decoded cells; a winner dominates even an unpromised opponent; a dominating nonwinner still needs verification; losers can cycle; weighted sampling nevertheless reaches the winner. These are substantially better explanatory choices than simply presenting a tournament lemma.

The remaining organization is less effective. The actual sampling-stage example arrives after the general pivot law, the full bounded-amplification recipe and the coupling estimate. It should arrive immediately after the joint-mass display. The reader could then recognize the success flag, conditional law and measured pivot before meeting the supplier that increases the chance of obtaining that same sample.

The amplification constants are the clearest weighting error. The paragraph derives `19/40`, weakens it to `9/40`, chooses a logarithmic trial count and explains the two success-probability regimes. The following argument consumes a much smaller interface: a fixed cap, high success above a threshold, certain failure at zero, and preservation of the conditional label law. The round-six disposition explicitly planned to move the recipe out, but it remains. The supplied de Wolf notes, chapter 7, offer a useful comparison: they develop the reflection geometry and general amplification mechanism, then separate variants and assumptions from the consuming application. The present article does not need another full proof of that standard supplier on its main route.

The actual board makes all its comparisons deterministic. That is excellent for an initial trace, but it no longer exposes the fractional-weight feature. The long record already supplies a carefully scoped abstract comparison example with weights `(11999/12000,0,0,1)` and a nonuniform conditional law. A direct optional link, or one small fractional row, would explain why preserving the law is more than merely finding some surviving column. This should not be misrepresented as another full addressed-certificate board.

The final cost paragraph should show its factors visibly. A table or two aligned products would reveal that the square-root cost belongs to sampling or verification, while the pivot count, number of comparisons and decoder accuracy contribute logarithms. The words that the actual counts are fixed must be corrected to bounds on the counts.

### The classical section: show the reduction where the reader needs it

The planted-pair figure is effective. It identifies the two places where the inputs can first differ, and its caption correctly resets the address law and blank cells. The independent-versus-dependent choice of `I` is also strong: it specifies the empty-discovery case and demonstrates why a uniform marginal is insufficient. The separately capped direct-product statement and the conditional-value proof are carefully separated, with the numerical AND example illustrating why only the average value decreases.

The main remaining teaching gap is the simulator. Its operational description is correct, but the decisive query-to-prediction step is still abstract. The record contains a short table showing an outside query, a charged target query, a harmless outside query and an attempted addressed-cell query whose coordinate supplies the output without reading the cell. It also shows the aborting branch. Bringing that existing trace onto the main route would teach more of the new lower bound than keeping all the standard amplification constants above. The present link should at least land on `#CL-simulator-example`, not merely on the preceding general definition.

The three budgets are now named correctly: `G` globally, `L` in total for one target column, and the direct product's `L` per instance. That successful repair should remain. The last averaging step should show the `1/m` multiplying the sum over columns, because this is where a novice is likely to introduce an unnecessary extra union bound. The contradiction should also explicitly connect the error requirement to average acceptance at least two thirds on planted inputs and at most one third on dummy inputs. These are short explanations of existing steps, not requests for new mathematics.

### Closing, visual presentation and what must survive

The exponent argument is mostly effective: it keeps `k` fixed, gives a counterexample to careless diagonal substitution, and states the order of choices explicitly. Its endpoint exercise must be repaired. Phrases such as “exactly the cube” should refer to the power exponent while acknowledging the logarithmic loss. The elementary floor threshold should remain distinct from the imported Forrelation threshold.

The ordinary AI-credit paragraph and bibliography should remain ordinary.

Figure 1's small internal text and Figure 3's angled calculation labels can be improved. Figure 2 splits the sentence explaining that the circuit is public across pages 4–5. The optional direct-product proof continues on the next page; a continuation label would help, but this is a lower priority than teaching the simulator. The revision should preserve the document's mathematical backbone: the negative Forrelation input, domain-bit board, dominating nonwinner, loser cycle, exact conditional sampling law, absorbing-state convention, joint success-and-cap event, independent planted column, fixed-parameter quantifier order, and the concrete formal-definition check. The next pass should redistribute explanation around these inferences, not add another layer of evidence administration.


## Reasoning record: substantial proof resource, costly navigation

### Overall verdict

The current reasoning record contains a substantially more usable mathematical argument than its accumulated review apparatus suggests. I could follow the construction, the quantum tournament, the capped simulator and the exponent comparison through the current Markdown. The strongest passages explain an actual mechanism: the winner's outgoing pointers disqualify its opponents; complementary comparisons halve the expected total weight; a stopped simulator guesses a cell address before reading the cell; a random planted column turns a sum into an average. Those passages should determine the document's shape. Instead, they repeatedly compete with restated setup, declaration correspondence, version history and prose written to prevent a reviewer from drawing an inference that the local mathematics already excludes. [Current record, §§1.1, 5.2, 6.4–6.5.]

I would use this as the proof repository behind the two shorter accounts, and I would continue reading it when auditing one of its difficult claims. I would not yet describe it as an efficiently navigable continuous research exposition. The remaining problem is not a missing theorem or an invitation to develop stronger mathematics. It is that too many paragraphs have to serve the continuous mathematical reader, the incoming link reader, the source auditor and the formalization reviewer simultaneously. The right editorial pass would distinguish those purposes more consistently using the organization already present. It would not create another layer of process labels.

This assessment is based on a sequential reading of all mathematical prose in the 6,314-line current Markdown, all 121 Appendix B claim statements and argument links, and the complete appendices through the disclosure. I did not treat the older PDF as current, rerun Lean or Comparator, or re-establish the execution receipts. No new mathematical counterexample is alleged in these findings. Scope ambiguities are called ambiguities; incorrect section labels and stale catalogue descriptions are called consistency defects.

### The opening should make one promise to its reader

The introduction succeeds when it says what makes this construction different. A winner has enough information in its own cell to reject every other candidate, including a candidate whose partial-function calls violate their promise. This gives the quantum argument an unusually robust comparison primitive. The lower bound then hides two kinds of changed coordinates, the erased zero and the correctly addressed cell. A reader can understand the proof's organizing tension before knowing the Forrelation supplier. [Current record, opening and §1.1.]

The same opening is less effective when it nearly completes the final exponent proof before introducing the function. The quantified theorem, fixed-k ratio, proof that a positive power outruns logarithms, infimum conclusion, fixed-k limitation, growing-k pointer and cubic corollary form an extended first conclusion. Section 7 later performs much of that work again. The order of quantifiers must remain near the theorem, because it controls the claim. The full ratio calculation needs one principal home. The introduction should tell the reader why the family yields an optimal universal exponent, then let the construction occupy the foreground.

The four reading routes are a real improvement over the earlier contradictory required/optional labels. They are still hard to execute. The construction row is an itinerary of many section ranges, followed by a paragraph that subtracts the circuit arithmetic, census and support remark, followed later by a dependency table and another routes paragraph. The record needs one reliable route, with links at the few meaningful stages, not three partly overlapping descriptions of how to read it. There is no need to delete the other information; its location should answer a question the reader is currently asking. [Current record, Reading guide and §1.1; previous-round plan, C1.]

The introductory lower-bound heuristic needs one precision adjustment. A lightly queried column does not acquire a simple conditional guarantee merely because its eventual query count is small. The proof uses a fixed-time joint event and then a union over times. The main body is careful about this, but the opening can still invite the interpretation that the main body rejects. A short indication of the event structure would be enough; the introduction does not need another full lemma.

### Models: retain the conventions, reduce implementation interruptions

Section 2 earns its existence. The same-algorithm quantifier, the all-input cap, the inclusive success threshold and the allowance of arbitrary finite biased mixtures are not decorative details. The OR example demonstrates that changing a seemingly harmless convention can change the numerical complexity. The one-sided-promise example makes false completion a different problem. The controlled-phase discussion explains the role of an idle or control facility by an elementary global-sign observation. These are good examples of explaining a definition through the mistake it rules out. [Current record, §§2.1–2.6.]

The section becomes less readable when the source representation is inserted into the mathematical definition at every turn. The `totalise` operation and wrapper filename are useful to a formalization reader, but they are not the most direct way to introduce the distinction between a total function and a partial function. The QLe disjunction looks like an unusual quantum model until the next paragraph explains the empty-coordinate representation problem. The explanation should precede the disjunction. The deferred-measurement proof similarly needs its stronger induction invariant before the auxiliary isometry is used: one map preserves the desired accepted mass, while another must intertwine the oracle.

These are local sequencing changes, not requests to suppress precision. The zero-query proof already demonstrates the desired balance until its last sentence turns to a former fidelity return and absent receipts. That information belongs to G.3, where its status can be understood beside the other semantic controls. Repeating it at the end of an elementary proof makes the reader doubt whether the ordinary argument has really finished.

### The Forrelation supplier needs a cleaner entry and exit

The direct Forrelation proof now announces its structure and credit well. It identifies the two distributions, first-block interpolation, derivative transport and tree estimate. It explains what comes from Bansal–Sinha and why the algebraic truncation is used here. The statement that a derivative of order k can contain higher uniform Fourier levels is an especially important clarification. The worked short chain shows the mechanism instead of only asserting a general identity. These are improvements worth protecting. [Current record, §§3.6–3.10.]

The section nevertheless begins several times. The function and normalization are introduced in §3.1, reintroduced in the lower-bound landing at §3.6 and collected again in §3.17. A self-contained linked proof needs a local statement, so wholesale deletion would harm the funnel. The solution is to identify a recap as a recap, keep it compact and allow the full ingredient theorem to be the clean exit. At present §3.16 gives detailed credit, §3.17 supplies that useful exit, and §3.18 reopens the source comparison yet again. The reader should leave with the three-property contract, not a third account of the literature roles.

Inside the derivative-growth argument, the main teaching opportunity is motivational order. The coefficient arrays and terminal polynomial process are introduced before the signed derivative sum they will represent is made explicit. A reader can reconstruct their purpose by the end, but should not have to. The proof should reveal its target before building the martingale machinery. The phrase “terminal chaos” adds another hurdle because the text has not established that vocabulary. The stopped-process treatment itself is valuable and should remain; removing it in pursuit of brevity would make the argument less reliable. [Current record, §3.11.] Compare [Girish–Tal–Wu, arXiv:2103.11604v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.11604v2), §1.3 and Lemma 1.9.

The promise-gap calculation has the same sequencing issue at a smaller scale. The argument eventually explains that the available yes-promise mass can be exponentially small in k, making constant error insufficient. That is the reason for amplification, and it should appear before the repetition calculation. The exact constants can then be read as meeting a known budget. The circuit-size calculation and final threshold also benefit from stating the purpose of deliberate slack: a jump from 337 to 800 is a convenient rounding, not a hidden new cost. None of this asks for tighter bounds.

The optional source comparisons should remain carefully scoped. The supplied Bansal–Sinha proof ending supports inspecting the indicated logarithmic conversion, but the record rightly distinguishes a defective displayed comparison from a false theorem. The SSW transfer is correctly separated from SSW's original random-orthogonal problem. Its sentence saying that the dependence on k disappears is too broad: the extra multiplicative amplification loss is canceled, while k remains in the exponent and dimension expression. [Current record, Appendix E; Bansal–Sinha, arXiv:2008.07003v3, final lower-bound proof; Sherstov–Storozhenko–Wu, arXiv:2008.10223v4, independent-work discussion and Fourier-growth corollaries.]

### Construction and quantum proof: emphasize the mechanisms already present

The construction gives a precise winning predicate, proves totality and uniqueness from that predicate, and invokes the realizing circuit only when verification requires locally checkable outputs. That order is correct and useful. The board example then shows what each class of test checks. Two local prose changes would improve it immediately: state the rule for the remaining cells as part of the mathematical example instead of making the checker its grammatical subject, and replace the ambiguous “all of them pass” after the count of 5,936 bits with an explicit reference to the tests. [Current record, §§4.2–4.4.]

The coherent-test section has three related interfaces with different costs. Before giving all of them, it should identify which interface is needed later. Otherwise the special uniform case can look like an available shortcut for the general coherently indexed application. The support remark now has the correct secondary role; its round-six repair should not be undone.

The quantum proof contains the clearest new mechanism but delays it with repeated setup. The amplification lemma is a single dense paragraph holding several independent guarantees. The tournament statement is followed by a comparison-model block, a parameter block and a proof opening that repeat its hypotheses. The theorem needs a clear statement and a four-stage proof map. It does not need the same definition of T, epsilon and dominance three times before the first update. [Current record, §§5.1–5.2.]
